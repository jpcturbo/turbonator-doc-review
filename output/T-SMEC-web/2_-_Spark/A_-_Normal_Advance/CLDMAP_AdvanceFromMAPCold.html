1. Overview & Purpose

CLDMAP defines the manifold pressure spark advance curve applied while engine coolant temperature remains below the warm operating threshold.

In cold engines, liquid fuel atomization is poor and flame propagation across the cold combustion chamber surfaces is slow. To maintain stable combustion, prevent cold hesitation, and ensure smooth warm-up drive-away, CLDMAP provides substantial ignition advance under manifold vacuum conditions, scaling from 20 degrees BTDC at deep vacuum down to 5 degrees of retard under positive boost pressure. This table functions as the cold-engine counterpart to the warm part-throttle and wide-open throttle timing curves.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cold Drive-Away Performance:
  • Raising values in CLDMAP adds ignition advance across the vacuum range, improving cold throttle response and engine pep before the engine reaches full operating temperature.
  • Avoid excessive advance in boost regions to prevent cold-engine detonation.
  • Lowering & Octane Margin:
  • Lowering table values retards cold timing, providing safety against cold detonation on low-octane fuel, though excessive reduction can cause cold hesitation or stumble.
  • Closed-Throttle Interaction:
  • At closed throttle, the cold MAP timing value is clamped from above by CLDLMT to prevent high idle flares.
  • 3-Bar MAP Rescaling:
  • Converting to a 3-bar MAP sensor requires recalculating the pressure breakpoints to align properly with vacuum and positive boost regions on the 44.1 psi absolute scale.

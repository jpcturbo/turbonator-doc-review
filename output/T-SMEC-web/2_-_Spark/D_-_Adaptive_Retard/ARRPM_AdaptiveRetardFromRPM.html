1. Overview & Purpose

ARRPM defines the RPM-indexed adaptive spark retard curve.

When persistent combustion detonation is detected while operating under boost, the engine controller increments an adaptive knock factor in background memory. In the spark advance routine, ARRPM is evaluated across engine speed, multiplied by the adaptive retard factor, and subtracted directly from the mechanical advance curve (GOVNER). This provides long-term learned timing reduction across the engine speed range, protecting the engine if fuel quality is substandard or operating conditions cause chronic detonation.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Subroutine & Line Numbers: DetermineMechanicalAdvanceFromRpm (T-SMEC_v19.asm:8019-8033), called from CalculateSparkAdvance_MM.
  • Gating Conditions:
  • Evaluated only when manifold pressure exceeds ARMPIC_AdaptiveRetard_MinMAPForAdjustment (cmpb ARMPIC; bls L1667).
  • Active when AdaptiveRetardIndex (RAM address 0x002B) is greater than zero.
  • Multiplication & Subtraction:
  • ARRPM_AdaptiveRetardFromRPM is looked up via EngineRpm_HB.
  • Multiplied by AdaptiveRetardIndex via 8x8 unsigned multiplication.
  • High byte of product is subtracted from mechanical advance in accumulator A (sba).

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Learned Retard Authority:
  • ARRPM specifies the upper ceiling of learned timing that can be pulled across engine speed; stock curve provides up to 4.0 degrees of retard around peak torque (2800 to 3600 RPM).
  • Disabling for Consistent Tuning:
  • Zeroing ARRPM eliminates learned timing reductions from RPM advance, ensuring dyno pulls and street logs reflect predictable, fixed table timing.
  • Octane Adaptation:
  • On dual-fuel or street cars that occasionally encounter lower-octane pump gasoline, retaining ARRPM allows the controller to safely adapt without permanent pinging.

1. Overview & Purpose

SSIPRD defines the low-engine-speed distributor pulse period threshold below which spark scatter idle stabilization is inhibited.

The engine controller evaluates the time elapsed between distributor pulses (TimerOverflowsBetweenDistPulses). In period-based timing logic, a larger period represents a slower engine speed. If an engine begins to stall and engine speed sags severely, rapidly dithering ignition timing can destabilize combustion and accelerate the stall. If the distributor pulse period is greater than or equal to SSIPRD (engine speed falls below approximately 565 RPM in factory calibrations), spark scatter is inhibited, allowing the controller to focus on maximum solid ignition advance and idle air opening to rescue the engine from stalling.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Mopar Performance Shift-Hang Calibration:
  • Setting SSIPRD to 65075 counts (hexadecimal FE33) inhibits spark scatter entirely on manual transmission cars, eliminating abrupt timing drops between shifts and keeping revs up for aggressive gear changes.
  • Anti-Stall Protection Floor:
  • Factory setting of 51 counts (~565 RPM) locks out spark scatter when the engine sags toward an impending stall, allowing base timing to catch the engine.
  • Period-vs-RPM Logic:
  • Remember that period is inversely proportional to RPM: larger numbers mean lower engine speed.

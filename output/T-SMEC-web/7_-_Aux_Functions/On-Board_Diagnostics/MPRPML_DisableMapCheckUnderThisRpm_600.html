1. Overview & Purpose

MPRPML defines the lower engine speed limit for the manifold absolute pressure sensor steady-state rationality validation window.

To confirm that the MAP sensor is operating accurately and providing trustworthy load signals, the engine controller validates the sensor reading against expected manifold pressure boundaries under steady idle and light cruising conditions. However, during engine cranking, initial startup flare, or unstable low-speed chugging below normal idle speed, manifold vacuum fluctuates wildly.

In factory calibrations, MPRPML is configured to 4800 counts, corresponding to exactly 600 RPM. If engine speed is below MPRPML, the controller suspends steady-state sensor validation, preventing false rationality errors during engine start.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Routine & Line Numbers: StoreAntiDwell (T-SMEC_v19.asm:7040-7063), instruction at line 7052.
  • Gating Conditions:
  • Engine running confirmed (b51_SparkEngineNotRunning clear at line 7040).
  • MAP delta below MPDELT at line 7047.
  • b45_MapStuck and b54_BigMapChange flags both clear.
  • Math & Comparison:
  • EngineRpm_HB loaded into register D at line 7051.
  • cmpD MPRPML_DisableMapCheckUnderThisRpm_600 at line 7052 compares 16-bit RPM against 4800 counts (600 RPM).
  • If lower (bcs), branches to L1595 at line 7053, bypassing assertion of b47_MapOK.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Camshaft & High Idle Tuning:
  • If installing performance camshafts that require an elevated idle target (e.g., 950 RPM), raise MPRPML from 600 RPM to 750 RPM to avoid erratic validation during lope.
  • Startup Stability:
  • Keeping MPRPML above cranking speed (600 RPM vs 200-250 RPM cranking) prevents false rationality judgments during engine startup.
  • Edge Cases:
  • Zeroing MPRPML forces validation during engine cranking when manifold vacuum is completely unstable.
  • Setting MPRPML higher than normal cruising RPM disables MAP steady-state validation entirely.

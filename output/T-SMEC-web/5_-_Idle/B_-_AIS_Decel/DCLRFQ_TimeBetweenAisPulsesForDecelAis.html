1. Overview & Purpose

DCLRFQ defines the minimum time delay that must elapse between successive stepper motor repositioning pulses during deceleration dashpot control.

When the vehicle decelerates with closed throttle, manifold pressure drops rapidly, triggering the decel dashpot routines to open the stepper motor to catch the descent. If the controller were permitted to command stepper movements on every single program loop execution, the stepper motor would outpace its mechanical response capabilities, dropping pulses and becoming physically desynchronized.

DCLRFQ acts as a pacing filter, enforcing a minimum settling delay between decel stepper motor movements.

In factory calibrations, DCLRFQ is set to 63 counts, enforcing a settling interval of approximately 700 milliseconds between decel stepper pulses.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Catching Rapid Decel Plunges:
  • Lower DCLRFQ from 63 to 35 to 45 counts on cars with lightweight flywheels to allow faster stepper motor intervention on throttle snap-shut.
  • Preventing Motor Desynchronization:
  • Do not lower DCLRFQ below 20 counts, as pulsing the coils faster than the motor pintle can move will cause skipped steps.
  • Factory Pacing:
  • Factory setting of 63 counts (~700 ms) provides conservative, reliable stepper motor tracking on standard rotating assemblies.

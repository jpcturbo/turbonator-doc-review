1. Overview & Purpose

PNDLTP defines the engine speed error threshold required before the controller triggers the automatic transmission Drive to Park/Neutral step reduction table.

When the driver shifts an automatic transmission out of Drive or Reverse into Park or Neutral, the hydraulic load of the torque converter is suddenly released from the crankshaft. If the idle air stepper motor remained in its loaded in-gear position, the sudden uncoupling of transmission drag would cause the engine to flare upward loudly into a high-RPM surge.

To prevent this flare, the ECU detects the RPM change upon shifting out of gear.

If the difference between target idle speed and actual engine speed exceeds PNDLTP, the controller advances its memory pointer to index the DRPNK closing step table.

In factory calibrations, PNDLTP is set to 160 counts, representing an RPM delta trigger threshold of approximately 80 RPM.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Eliminating Neutral Shift Flare:
  • Lower PNDLTP to 50 to 65 RPM if the engine flares upward loudly when shifting out of gear into Park or Neutral.
  • Threshold Minimum:
  • Do not set PNDLTP below 30 RPM to avoid false triggering from natural idle speed oscillations.
  • Complementary Trigger:
  • Direct counterpart to PNDLT, governing the opposite shift transition.

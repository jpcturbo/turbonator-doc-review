1. Overview & Purpose

FANTMP1 defines the engine coolant temperature threshold required to energize the radiator cooling fan when the vehicle is traveling at highway speeds above 45 MPH.

At higher road speeds, natural forward ram airflow through the front fascia and radiator core provides substantial convective heat rejection without needing the electric cooling fan. To conserve electrical power and prolong fan motor life, the engine controller raises the temperature threshold required to start the fan when cruising at highway speeds. In factory calibrations, FANTMP1 is set to 232 counts, corresponding to approximately 204 °F. If engine coolant temperature exceeds this point at highway speed, the controller energizes the fan relay to assist cooling under heavy sustained cruise loads.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Thermostat Matching:
  • For 180 °F thermostats, lower FANTMP1 from factory 204 °F down to 192 °F to 196 °F.
  • For 160 °F thermostats, lower FANTMP1 down to 176 °F to 180 °F.
  • Hysteresis Relationship:
  • Maintain a 4 to 6 °F spread above FANTMP2 to prevent rapid cycling of the fan relay at cruising speeds.

5. Calibration Notes & System Dependencies

  • Indexed Offset Layout: Stored at offset +0 of the 4-byte fan temperature block (FANTMP1, FANTMP2, FANTMP3, FANTMP4).
  • Companion Turn-Off Parameter: Paired directly with FANTMP2_FanTurnOffTempAbove45MPH for high-speed hysteresis.
  • Speed Inversion: Speed pulse period VehicleSpeed_HB is inversely proportional to road speed (smaller pulse period counts = higher vehicle speed in MPH).

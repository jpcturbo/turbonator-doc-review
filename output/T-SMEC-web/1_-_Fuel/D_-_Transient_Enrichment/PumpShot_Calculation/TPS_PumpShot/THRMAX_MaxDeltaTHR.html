1. Overview & Purpose

THRMAX defines the maximum allowable throttle position sensor delta voltage used to compute the throttle-based acceleration enrichment pump shot.

When the accelerator is rapidly floored from idle, the throttle sensor voltage can jump by more than 3.5 volts in a fraction of a second. If this entire voltage swing were multiplied by the enrichment slope, the controller would inject a massive flood of fuel that would bog or drown the engine. THRMAX clamps the effective throttle opening delta after the trigger threshold is subtracted, capping the maximum allowable transient pulse width.

The factory setting of 35 counts limits the active calculation range to approximately 0.70 volts above the trigger threshold.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Curing Rich Tip-in Bog: Lowering THRMAX limits the maximum volume of fuel that a rapid throttle depression can deliver, which is especially critical when running high-flow aftermarket fuel injectors that deliver substantially more fuel per millisecond.
  • Eliminating Exhaust Smoke: If aggressive off-idle throttle stomps produce a momentary rich stumble with black exhaust smoke, lowering THRMAX cures the bog.
  • High-Flow Manifolds: Raising THRMAX permits larger transient pump shots during violent wide-open throttle transitions, which may be required on engines with high-flow intake manifolds or dry nitrous transitions.
  • Injector Rescaling Rule: When doubling injector flow capacity, reduce THRMAX by roughly 25 to 40 percent alongside POSSLP.

1. Overview & Purpose

LOTRPM sets the minimum engine speed threshold required for MAP-based deceleration lean-out to operate.

When coasting down toward a stop, the engine controller must restore full fueling before the engine reaches idle speed to prevent rough running, RPM dipping, or stalling. If engine speed drops below LOTRPM, the controller clears the deceleration lean-out factor and resumes standard fuel delivery.

The factory setting of 62 counts corresponds to approximately 1984 RPM, ensuring that fuel delivery is fully normalized well before the engine settles into its target idle range.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Preventing Clutch-in Stalling: In manual transmission setups with lightweight flywheels, raising LOTRPM to 2200 to 2400 RPM gives the engine more time to recover full fueling, eliminating RPM dipping and stalling when pushing the clutch pedal in.
  • Automatic Transmission Optimization: In automatic transmission vehicles, LOTRPM can be lowered closer to 1600 to 1800 RPM to maximize fuel cutoff duration and fuel economy.
  • Lower Limit Rule: Do not set LOTRPM below the target idle speed, as cutting fuel during idle recovery causes stalling.
  • Camshaft Matching: High-overlap performance camshafts with unstable low-RPM vacuum benefit from raising LOTRPM to prevent rough low-speed coast-down.

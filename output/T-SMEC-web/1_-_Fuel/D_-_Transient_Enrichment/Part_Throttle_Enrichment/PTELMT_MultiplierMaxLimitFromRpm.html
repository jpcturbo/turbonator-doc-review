1. Overview & Purpose

PTELMT establishes the engine speed-dependent ceiling for part-throttle open-loop fuel enrichment.

During sustained part-throttle acceleration, the controller continually increments the enrichment multiplier; however, an uncontrolled fuel multiplier would eventually over-fuel the engine and wash cylinder walls. PTELMT defines the maximum percentage multiplier allowed as engine speed climbs.

The factory calibration sets an allowable enrichment ceiling of approximately 12 percent at 2300 RPM, scaling up progressively to 33 percent at 5300 RPM. This ceiling is subsequently modulated by PTEADJ to account for manifold boost pressure.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Preventing Lean Boost Pulls: Raise PTELMT values if wideband data logging shows that the engine runs leaner than desired during long part-throttle pulls in boost.
  • High-Performance Detonation Margin: Raising the limit allows the open-loop enrichment multiplier to ramp to higher fuel percentages, providing extra detonation margin on high-performance turbocharged builds.
  • Curing Rich Part-Throttle Bog: Lower PTELMT values if the engine feels sluggish, bogs rich, or emits black smoke when holding moderate throttle under boost, which commonly occurs after installing high-flow aftermarket injectors.
  • RPM Profile Shaping: Shape the curve to provide lower limits at low RPM where airflow is low, tapering up to maximum enrichment at high RPM where volumetric efficiency peaks.

  • Direct Ceiling Role: Acts as the master upper clamp for Timer_OpenLoopFraction.

T-SMEC Engine Modifications Tuning Guide

A practical, hands-on calibration guide for Chrysler Turbo Single Module Engine Controller (T-SMEC / Turbonator-SMEC) applications. While MPTune automatically handles the base math for injector scaling and MAP sensor conversion slopes (MAPMULT and MAPADD), it does not perform hands-on fine tuning, nor does it automate compensations for free-flowing exhausts, enlarged throttle bodies, or radical camshaft profiles. This guide breaks down each modification individually: which tables and constants to adjust in MPTune, why they need to change, and real-world calibration best practices.

Hardware: Chrysler 2.2L & 2.5L Turbo (Turbo I / Turbo II / Turbo IV) ECU: Single Module Engine Controller (T-SMEC, 1988–1989) Calibration Tools: MPTune & MPScan Format: Practical Tuner Reference (No Raw Hex / Real-World Units)

Automated MPTune Setup vs. Tuner Responsibility

When setting up a calibration for a modified engine in MPTune, it is essential to distinguish between what the software calculates automatically and what requires hands-on calibration:

What MPTune Wizards Automate

  • Base Fuel Pulsewidth Rescaling: Scales down the main fuel tables (WOTTBL and MAPTBL) proportionally based on original vs. new injector flow ratings.
  • MAP Sensor Transfer Slopes: Recalculates the primary voltage-to-pressure conversion constants (MAPMULT and MAPADD) and stretches the pressure axes on your fuel, spark, and boost tables for 3-bar or 4-bar sensors.

What Requires Hands-On Tuner Attention

  • Injector Latency & Dead Time (BATOFF): Modern aftermarket injectors (Bosch EV14, Siemens Deka) open much faster than stock pintle injectors; MPTune does not adjust dead time.
  • Adaptive Learning Floor (PWMIN): Cruise pulsewidths on big injectors drop below the factory 1.10 ms floor, locking out adaptive fuel trim cell updates unless scaled down.
  • Closed-Loop O2 Ramp Rates (SECRMP / PRIRMP): Oversized injectors inject too much fuel per ramp step tick, requiring continuous ramp rates to be softened to eliminate wideband idle/cruise hunting.
  • Transient Tip-In Pumpshot (POSSLP / POSTRG): Bigger throttle bodies create massive manifold pressure delta that requires enhanced pumpshot, while massive injectors require trimmed duration.
  • High-Boost Ignition Retard: MPTune stretches the MAP axis for 3-bar/4-bar sensors but leaves timing flat above 14.7 psi; tuners must manually slope ignition advance downward.
  • Physical Airflow & Overlap Dynamics: Camshaft overlap ruins low-RPM idle vacuum, shifting operating cells into cruise regions and altering knock sensor noise thresholds (DLTKNK).

1. Larger Exhaust & Catalytic Converter Removal (2.5" / 3" Mandrel Systems)

Physical modifications: 2.5" or 3" mandrel downpipe, high-flow catalytic converter or test pipe, and free-flowing cat-back system.
Exhaust & Boost Control

What Happens to the Engine

The factory exhaust system creates significant post-turbine backpressure. Replacing it with a 2.5" or 3" mandrel-bent system and eliminating restrictive catalytic converters dramatically alters the expansion ratio across the turbine wheel:

  • Significantly Faster Turbo Spool-Up: The increased pressure differential across the turbine wheel allows the turbocharger to spool 300 to 500 RPM sooner, building full boost with much less throttle pedal input.
  • Exhaust Reality — Solenoid Control vs. Manual Boost Controllers (MBC): Many tuners in the Chrysler turbo community use mechanical ball-and-spring manual boost controllers (grainger valves), which completely bypass the SMEC wastegate solenoid. If using an MBC, the ECU wastegate tables do not regulate boost. However, if retaining factory ECU solenoid control, the faster turbine spool can cause a transient 1 to 3 psi boost overshoot before the electronic control loop can bleed off actuator pressure.
  • Enhanced Top-End Scavenging: Greatly reduced exhaust manifold and downpipe backpressure evacuates residual combustion gases during valve overlap, increasing volumetric efficiency above 4,800 RPM and reducing in-cylinder combustion temperatures.
  • Potential Boost Creep: On aggressive 3" exhaust setups with stock small wastegate swingvalves, the wastegate port may be physically unable to bypass enough exhaust gas at high RPM, leading to mechanical boost creep independent of ECU duty cycle.

✓ Manual Boost Controller (MBC) Reality Check: If using a Grainger valve or dual-stage ball-and-spring MBC under the hood, the SMEC wastegate solenoid is disconnected from the wastegate actuator. Solenoid duty cycle tables (DCWOT, DCWADJ) have zero influence over boost. Set your boost mechanically on the road or dyno.
✓ Check for Mechanical Boost Creep: Do a 3rd-gear wide-open pull from 2,500 to 6,000 RPM. If boost holds at 14 psi until 4,500 RPM and then steadily climbs to 18+ psi near redline with DCWOT at minimum duty cycle, the stock Chrysler wastegate hole in the turbine swingvalve is aerodynamically overwhelmed by the 3" downpipe. Porting the wastegate hole or installing a 2.5"/3" TU SV swingvalve is required to resolve creep.
✓ Verify High-RPM Wideband AFR: Watch your wideband gauge above 5,000 RPM. If the AFR drifts leaner than 12.0:1 on pump gas, bump PEFTBL at the 5,000, 5,728, and 6,208 RPM breakpoints by 3% to 5% to restore safe enrichment.

2. Bigger Throttle Body (52mm / 58mm vs. Stock 46mm)

Physical modifications: 52mm (Mopar / Super 60 / 3.0L V6 swap), 58mm (aftermarket / billet), or custom enlarged plenum inlet.
Transient Fuel & Airflow

What Happens to the Engine

The stock Chrysler turbo throttle body has a 46mm bore (1,661 mm² area). Upgrading to 52mm increases cross-sectional area by 28% (2,123 mm²), while a 58mm increases area by 59% (2,642 mm²). This dramatic increase in bore diameter causes major physical changes:

  • The Classic Tip-In Stumble (Off-Idle Bog): Because cross-sectional flow area increases with the square of the radius, opening a 52mm or 58mm throttle blade just 3° to 5° rushes far more ambient air into the intake plenum than the stock 46mm throttle plate. Intake manifold vacuum collapses almost instantaneously from 18 inHg down to 2 inHg. The engine receives a sudden mass airflow surge before standard fuel delivery can respond, causing a momentary severe lean spike (16:1–18:1 AFR) and a noticeable engine stumble.
  • Idle Bypass Air Saturation & Closed-Throttle Leakage: A larger throttle plate has a greater circumference, allowing more baseline air leakage past the perimeter even when fully seated against the stop screw. This reduces the required opening of the Automatic Idle Speed (AIS) stepper motor. If base idle stop screw mechanical alignment is not reset, the AIS will close completely against its seat (0 steps), and engine idle will hang stubbornly high (1,100–1,400 RPM).
  • Non-Linear Throttle Response: Manifold absolute pressure equalizes to atmospheric pressure at approximately 45% to 55% throttle pedal angle instead of the factory 75% to 80%, making the engine feel like an "on/off light switch" unless part-throttle boost targets and transient enrichments are remapped.
Understanding Dual-Pumpshot Architecture in the SMEC
The Chrysler SMEC executes two distinct transient fuel enrichment algorithms in parallel:
1. TPS-Based Pumpshot (POSSLP & THRMAX): Fires immediate asynchronous or synchronous supplemental fuel based on the rate of throttle position voltage change (ΔTPS).
2. MAP-Based Pumpshot (AESLOP & MAPMAX): Supplies transient fuel based on the rapid collapse of manifold vacuum (ΔMAP).
When installing a larger throttle body, increasing TPS-based pumpshot (POSSLP) is the primary cure for off-idle bog, while lowering the trigger threshold (POSTRG) catches small throttle movements.

Tuner's Field Procedure — Setting Mechanical Base Idle on a 52mm / 58mm Throttle Body

  1. Bring the engine to full operating temperature (180°F–195°F coolant temp confirmed in MPScan).
  2. With the engine running at warm idle, unplug the 4-wire AIS stepper motor electrical harness connector. This locks the stepper pintle in its current position.
  3. Turn the mechanical throttle stop screw (using an Allen key / torx) until baseline engine speed sits at 650–700 RPM (roughly 100–150 RPM below your target idle speed). Ensure the throttle blade does not bind in the bore when closed.
  4. Connect MPScan and verify throttle position sensor (TPS) voltage with throttle closed. It must read between 0.60V and 0.85V. If it reads higher than 0.90V, elongate the TPS mounting holes slightly to rotate the sensor back into range.
  5. Shut off the engine, reconnect the AIS motor harness, and disconnect the battery for 60 seconds. This clears the SMEC's volatile RAM and forces the controller to learn the new minimum throttle voltage (MINTHR) upon the next ignition cycle.

3. Bigger Injectors (+20%, +40%, 72+ pph) [Fine-Tuning Guidelines]

Injector upgrades: +20% (~42 lb/hr, Mopar/Bosch), +40% (~52–55 lb/hr, Siemens Deka/Super 60), and 72+ pph (~750–850 cc/min modern high-impedance).
Fine-Tuning Only (MPTune Handles Base Scaling)

What MPTune Automatically Scales vs. Tuner Fine-Tuning

In MPTune, selecting Scaling → Injectors → Flow Rate rescales the base fuel pulsewidth tables (WOTTBL and MAPTBL). This mathematical division works well as a foundation. However, physical injectors have electromechanical characteristics that do not scale linearly with flow rate:

  • Electromechanical Dead Time Latency (BATOFF): Injector dead time (the time required for the magnetic coil to lift the pintle off its seat) is a fixed mechanical property (typically 0.6 to 1.2 ms), not a percentage of fuel flow. Modern high-flow injectors (such as Bosch EV14 or modern disc-type injectors) open significantly faster than 1980s factory Bosch pintle injectors. If dead time is not corrected, warm idle pulsewidths will be severely miscalibrated, causing idle AFR to swing lean or rich whenever system voltage changes (e.g., when the radiator fan engages).
  • The Adaptive Learning Pulsewidth Floor Lockout (PWMIN): The SMEC firmware contains an adaptive learning lockout floor (PWMIN) calibrated to 1.10 ms (275 counts) in stock files. This floor prevents the ECU from updating long-term adaptive fuel memory trims during deceleration when pulsewidths drop to near zero. On +40% or 72+ pph injectors, normal warm idle and light highway cruising pulsewidths drop into the 0.80 ms to 1.05 ms range. Because actual pulsewidth falls below stock PWMIN, the SMEC permanently locks out adaptive fuel trim updates, preventing closed-loop long-term trims from adapting. Scaling PWMIN down restores full factory adaptive learning.
  • Closed-Loop O2 Ramp Step Hunting (SECRMP / PRIRMP): Because a 72 pph injector sprays more than double the fuel per millisecond of pintle lift compared to a stock 33 pph injector, standard closed-loop step corrections inject too much fuel per cycle. The factory primary idle step (PRIRMP) is already at the single-step hardware minimum (1 count rich / 1 count lean) and cannot be lowered further. Therefore, closed-loop hunting must be eliminated in SECRMP, reducing the secondary integration rate from +3/-3 counts down to +1/-1 count per tick.
  • Transient Over-Fueling Bog on Throttle Tip-In: Because upgraded injectors deliver vastly more fuel per pulse, factory throttle pumpshot (POSSLP) and cranking fuel tables squirt too much fuel, causing a rich bog and black exhaust smoke upon rapid acceleration.

Tuner's Field Procedure — Dialing in Injector Latency (BATOFF) Using Electrical Load Testing

Because injector flow rate governs pulsewidth duration while dead time governs voltage offset, you can dial in BATOFF with surgical accuracy on an idling engine:

  1. Warm the engine fully to operating temperature and let it idle in closed-loop or locked open-loop (targeting 14.7:1 AFR). Ensure system voltage is steady at alternator charging level (~14.0V–14.4V).
  2. Observe the wideband AFR while suddenly engaging all high-draw electrical accessories: switch headlights on high beam, turn heater blower motor to max speed, turn on the rear window defroster, and allow the radiator cooling fan to cycle on. System voltage will momentarily sag from ~14.2V down to ~12.2V–12.8V.
  3. If AFR swings LEAN when voltage drops: The injector dead time offset is too small (the injector is opening slower than the ECU thinks). Increase BATOFF values in the 12.0V–13.0V cells.
  4. If AFR swings RICH when voltage drops: The injector dead time offset is too large. Decrease BATOFF values in the 12.0V–13.0V cells.
  5. When BATOFF is perfectly calibrated, wideband AFR will remain rock steady at 14.7:1 regardless of what electrical loads turn on or off.

4. 3-Bar and 4-Bar MAP Sensors [Fine-Tuning Guidelines]

Sensor conversions: Mopar Super 60 / GM 3-Bar (~29 psi gauge boost) and GM / Omnipower 4-Bar (~44 psi gauge boost).
Fine-Tuning Only (MPTune Handles Transfer Slope)

What Happens When Converting to a Higher Pressure MAP Sensor

The Chrysler SMEC features an on-board 8-bit Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) that maps the 0.0V to 5.0V sensor output into 256 discrete digital counts (0 to 255). Expanding the sensor pressure range directly alters resolution:

  • Digital Resolution Trade-Off: On a factory 2-bar sensor (0 to 29.4 psia), each digital count represents approximately 0.115 psi (0.23 inHg). On a 3-bar sensor (0 to 44.1 psia), each count represents 0.173 psi (0.35 inHg). On a 4-bar sensor (0 to 58.8 psia), each count expands to 0.230 psi (0.47 inHg). In deep idle vacuum (16 to 20 inHg), the engine's entire idle and light-cruise operating zone lives within a compressed span of only 25 to 35 ADC steps.
  • Vacuum Step Jitter & Idle Hunting: Because each sensor count represents larger real-world pressure steps, any electrical noise or intake plenum pressure pulsation can toggle the ECU between adjacent table cells. If the fuel or ignition timing tables have steep ramps in the deep vacuum region, this 1-count flicker will cause timing and fueling to bounce back and forth, resulting in an erratic idle hunt or low-speed bucking.
  • Critical Tuner Responsibility — Spark Retard in High Boost: When MPTune converts a calibration for a 3-bar or 4-bar MAP sensor, it updates the voltage transfer slope constants (MAPMULT and MAPADD) and stretches the pressure axis of your fuel and spark tables. However, MPTune does NOT automatically retard high-boost ignition timing! In factory files, full-advance timing at 14.7 psi is typically 18° to 22°. When converted to 3-bar, MPTune holds that 18°–22° advance flat all the way out to 29 psi! The tuner must manually build a descending ignition slope in WOTMAP to pull timing as boost climbs.
  • Part-Throttle Boost Open-Loop Transition Threshold (BOOSTP): In stock 2-bar files, BOOSTP is configured to 255 counts (14.7 psi boost). On a 3-bar sensor, 255 counts corresponds to ~29.4 psi boost! Leaving BOOSTP at 255 counts would allow the ECU to linger in closed-loop stoichiometric fuel control under boost. It must be rescaled downward to ensure reliable open-loop transition at ~0 psi gauge pressure.
High Priority Safety Notice: Manually Shape High-Boost Ignition Timing (WOTMAP)
Never flash a 3-bar or 4-bar calibration without inspecting the full-throttle spark advance curve (WOTMAP). Running 20° of ignition advance at 25 psi on pump gas will produce severe detonation. Insert new table breakpoints at 18 psi, 22 psi, 26 psi, and 30 psi, sloping advance downward to safe levels (12°–16° total advance).

5. Generic Performance Camshaft (Higher Lift, Longer Duration, Tighter LSA)

Camshaft upgrades: Mopar Super 60, FWD-P Enforcer / F-Series, Taft, or custom high-duration turbo grinds.
VE Rotation & Idle Vacuum Dynamics

What Happens to the Engine

Performance camshafts increase valve lift and duration while often narrowing Lobe Separation Angle (LSA). These geometric changes significantly alter engine breathing across the RPM band:

  • Severe Drop in Idle Manifold Vacuum: Extended valve overlap causes intake reversion at low engine speeds. Idle manifold vacuum drops from factory stock 18–20 inHg down to 10–14 inHg (higher absolute manifold pressure).
  • The "Cruise Cell at Idle" Dilemma: Because the SMEC measures engine load via manifold pressure (Speed-Density), an engine idling at 12–14 inHg falls directly into the ECU's light-cruise fuel and ignition cells. The ECU assumes the vehicle is driving down the road under moderate load, delivering too much fuel and sluggish timing. This results in a rolling, hunting idle and fouled spark plugs unless idle fuel cells are leaned out.
  • Table Breakpoint Dynamics — The PEFTBL RPM Clamp: In Chrysler SMEC calibrations, the Pumping Efficiency table (PEFTBL) establishes volumetric efficiency across engine speed. The lowest RPM breakpoint in factory calibrations is typically 2,000 or 2,400 RPM. The SMEC firmware automatically clamps engine speeds below the lowest breakpoint to that first table entry. Therefore, changing PEFTBL has zero effect on idle AFR — idle fueling is governed exclusively by base pulsewidth (MAPTBL) and closed-loop O2 trims!
  • Premature Part-Throttle Enrichment (RPMMAP): Because the camshaft operates at higher absolute manifold pressure at all part-throttle cruise speeds, the engine can cross the factory part-throttle enrichment threshold (RPMMAP) prematurely, dumping fuel and hurting fuel economy during light highway cruising.
  • Acoustic Valvetrain Chatter & False Knock (DLTKNK): Stiffer aftermarket valve springs, steep ramp lobes, and mechanical valvetrain noise create harmonic vibration through the cylinder head that the factory knock sensor mistakes for detonation. The SMEC responds by pulling ignition timing (knock retard) under boost when no real detonation exists.
The Classic "PEFTBL Rotation" Rule for Camshaft Tuning
When tuning for a performance camshaft in Chrysler turbo engines, tuners universally describe recalibrating PEFTBL as rotating the table counter-clockwise:
• Low RPM (1,500–2,800 RPM): Drop values by 4% to 8% (cures soggy low-end throttle response and rich bogging).
• Mid RPM (3,200–4,000 RPM): Maintain near stock baseline values.
• High RPM (4,800–6,500 RPM): Raise values by 6% to 12%+ to feed the massive volumetric efficiency gains delivered by the cam's extended duration.

Tuner's Field Procedure — Validating Valvetrain Mechanical Noise with Det-Cans

Before raising DLTKNK to eliminate knock retard, build a simple mechanical det-can: bolt an eyelet fitting to a cylinder head intake bolt, attach 1/4" copper tubing running through the firewall, and connect it via rubber fuel hose to industrial acoustic earmuffs. Mechanical valvetrain chatter from aggressive camshaft lobes produces a rhythmic, steady sewing-machine clicking that rises smoothly with engine RPM. True detonation sounds like crisp, erratic, high-frequency bacon sizzling or sharp gravel rattling in a tin can. Only raise DLTKNK high enough to filter out the sewing-machine clatter, ensuring real detonation remains 100% protected.

1. Overview & Purpose

WSGRPM_AllowedBoost_FromRpm defines the baseline target boost pressure curve as a function of engine RPM.

In the Chrysler Logic Module boost control architecture, target boost is not a fixed static number; it is calculated dynamically to match the compressor map and torque curve of the engine. WSGRPM forms the initial foundation of this target: it commands lower boost at low RPM to prevent turbocharger compressor surge and driveline strain, and tapers or holds boost at higher RPMs where cylinder filling and turbocharger efficiency are optimal.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • The Foundation of Boost Targeting:
  • In CalcTargetBoost_MM, WSGRPM is the very first table queried.
  • Engine speed high-byte (EngineRpm_HB) is multiplied by 2 and looked up in WSGRPM.
  • The resulting boost value is stored in scratch memory (Temp4) as the initial baseline boost goal.
  • Subsequent tables (WSGTIM, WSGCLT, WSGCHG) can only reduce this boost goal via LookupAndKeepSmallerBoostGoal.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Target vs. Actual Boost: WSGRPM sets the target goal (in psi above baro). The wastegate duty cycle algorithm (DCWOT) modulates the wastegate solenoid to bring manifold absolute pressure up to match this target plus barometric pressure.
  • Raising WSGRPM:
  • Increases targeted boost pressure.
  • To raise boost to 14 psi, 18 psi, or higher, raise the Y-axis boost values in WSGRPM.
  • Crucial Prerequisite: Overboost fuel shutoff limits (MPSHFA / MPSHFT) must also be raised, or the ECU will cut fuel when boost exceeds the factory overboost ceiling!
  • Compressor Surge Prevention: Keep boost moderate under 2800 RPM to avoid putting large Garrett or Mitsubishi turbos into surge.

5. Calibration Notes & System Dependencies

  • Scaling: Boost is expressed in gauge pressure above barometric pressure (psig). At sea level (14.7 psia), a table value of 12.0 psi targets 14.7 + 12.0 = 26.7 ext psia ($12 ext{ psig}$ on your boost gauge).

1. Overview & Purpose

EPPMUL_FuelStartFuelPerEPP provides cranking fuel decay as engine revolutions accumulate during prolonged cranking.

When a driver turns the key to start the car, the initial 1 to 3 engine revolutions require heavy fuel delivery to wet cold intake runners. However, if the engine fails to start immediately and the starter continues to turn the engine over for 5 to 10 seconds, delivering that initial heavy fuel pulse continuously will quickly flood the spark plugs with liquid fuel. EPPMUL tracks the running count of Engine Position Pulses (EPPs) during the cranking event and progressively scales down cranking fuel pulse width as cranking drags on, preventing engine flooding.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

EPPMUL is looked up during every injection event in Start Mode: * Lookup Input: Counter_FallingEdgeEPP_MaxFF (running tally of distributor sync pulses since cranking began, capped at 255). * Action: The resulting scaling multiplier ($B$) is passed to ScaleXbySignedB_ReturnD, reducing Temp0 proportionally.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Increasing Table Values (Less Fuel Decay Over Time):
  • Effect: Keeps cranking pulse width richer for longer during extended cranking.
  • Real-World Feel: Helpful for stubborn cold starts on E85, alcohol fuels, or low-compression engines where prolonged cranking is required to build sufficient compression heat before the fuel ignites.
  • Decreasing Table Values (More Rapid Fuel Decay):
  • Effect: Cuts cranking fuel down more quickly as cranking continues.
  • Real-World Feel: Aggressively protects against fouled spark plugs if the engine doesn't catch on the first few cranks.

5. Calibration Notes & System Dependencies

  • EPP Count to Revolutions: On 4-cylinder Chrysler 2.2L/2.5L engines, 4 EPP pulses equal 2 crankshaft revolutions (1 complete 4-stroke cycle). Thus, 33 pulses represents approximately 8.25 full crankshaft rotations of full fuel before decay begins.

1. Overview & Purpose

RTRPME_RetardFromTPSEnableAboveThisRPM defines the minimum engine speed required before the ECU will apply ignition timing retard on rapid throttle openings (CheckForTPSRetard).

At very low engine speeds (below ~2,500 RPM), engine volumetric efficiency is lower and turbocharger transient spool is slower, so tip-in fueling is adequately covered by base acceleration pump shots. Furthermore, pulling ignition timing at lower engine speeds can severely impede turbo spool-up and cause the vehicle to bog or shudder. RTRPME confines the rapid TPS retard to mid- and high-RPM operations where turbocharger response is instant and high cylinder pressures make tip-in knock a critical concern.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

RTRPME is the second condition checked in CheckForTPSRetard: 1. RPM Gate: Engine speed high byte (EngineRpm_HB) must be strictly greater than RTRPME (stock 0x27 = ~2,496 RPM). 2. Coupled Conditions: - MAP must exceed RTMAPE (~1.5 psi boost). - Average TPS voltage must exceed RTTPSE. - \DeltaTPS > 0.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Increasing the Value (Higher RPM Threshold):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Delays TPS transient retard to higher RPMs.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Improves low-to-mid RPM throttle responsiveness and off-corner punch, but increases knock risk if aggressive spool-up occurs around 2,500–3,000 RPM.
  • Decreasing the Value (Lower RPM Threshold):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Allows TPS transient retard to trigger at lower engine speeds.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Adds knock protection for fast-spooling small turbochargers (such as stock Garrett T03 or Mitsubishi TE04H), but if set too low, can cause sluggish tip-in feel during low-speed gear shifts.

1. Overview & Purpose

SPKLMT_TotalAdvanceAllowed is the hard maximum ceiling curve on total ignition timing across engine speed.

Under light throttle cruising in deep vacuum (high HOTMAP advance) combined with high RPM (high GOVNER advance), the theoretical mathematical sum of RPM timing plus MAP advance could exceed 50° to 60° of advance. Running excessive timing—even under very light load—can cause misfires, cross-firing in the distributor cap, rotor phasing alignment problems, and negative work on the piston during compression. SPKLMT acts as a final safety limiter: if the total combined ignition timing exceeds the value in SPKLMT at that RPM, it is clamped down to SPKLMT.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

SPKLMT is the final check performed in CalculateSparkAdvance_MM immediately before timing is converted to timer counter ticks: * Evaluated Input: EngineRpm_HB. * Clamping Action: If Temp0 (total combined timing) > SPKLMT(RPM), Temp0 is forced to SPKLMT(RPM).

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Increasing Table Values (Raising the Ceiling):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Allows higher total timing during light-load high-RPM highway cruising.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Can improve fuel economy slightly on light cruise, but gains diminish past ~42–45° on typical 8-valve 2.2L/2.5L combustion chambers.
  • Decreasing Table Values (Lowering the Ceiling):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Imposes a lower absolute cap on timing across all operating conditions.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Prevents rotor crossfire and ensures safety, but setting it too low (e.g., <35° at 3,000 RPM) caps part-throttle cruising advance prematurely, hurting fuel economy and increasing cruise exhaust gas temperatures.
  • Stock Curve Reference:
  • In stock calibrations, SPKLMT starts around 24–28° at low RPM and ramps up to roughly 42–46° at 3,200+ RPM.

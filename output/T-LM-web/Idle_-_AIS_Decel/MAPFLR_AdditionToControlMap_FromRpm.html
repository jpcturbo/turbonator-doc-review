1. Overview & Purpose

MAPFLR (AISAdditionToControlMap_FromRpm) defines a target manifold absolute pressure floor as a function of engine RPM (EngineRpm_HB).

During closed-throttle deceleration, the engine acts as a powerful vacuum pump. At high RPM, manifold vacuum can easily exceed 25-28 inHg (< 15 kPa). Running the engine in this deep vacuum zone leads to high oil consumption past rings, plug fouling, and unstable re-ignition. MAPFLR establishes a synthetic target MAP floor that rises with RPM. The ECU modulates the AIS motor steps to keep live manifold pressure at or above this calibrated floor during decel.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Evaluated inside CalculateAisPosition_MM at line 3551.
  • Indexed by high byte of engine speed (EngineRpm_HB).
  • Multiplied by coolant temperature factor MPTEMP and barometric factor BARFLR.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • High RPM Decel Backfire / Burble: If the car bangs or backfires loudly in the exhaust when letting off the gas at high RPM (3,500 - 5,000 RPM), raising MAPFLR forces the AIS to admit more bypass air, raising manifold pressure and preventing extreme vacuum fuel vaporization.
  • Aggressive Engine Braking: To maximize engine braking on manual transmission road race cars, reduce MAPFLR so the engine pulls deeper vacuum.

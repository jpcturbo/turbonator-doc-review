1. Overview & Purpose

MPHREF (SwitchPointForDecelIdleAisControl) is the critical speed threshold (stock calibration = 6 MPH / 0x06) defining the operational boundary between stationary curb idle regulation and moving deceleration control.

When the vehicle is moving above MPHREF, the ECU treats closed-throttle events as trailing-throttle vehicle deceleration, engaging dashpot anti-stall airflow curves (DLYTIM, TIMEDC, MAPFLR). When vehicle speed drops below MPHREF (with a built-in 1.5 MPH hysteresis deadband), the ECU transitions to stationary curb idle control (DLYTIM2, FWDAIS, REVAIS). This clean separation prevents rolling vehicles from surging at stop signs or dragging their idle motors while coasting in gear.

  • Evaluated in SetTPSFlags_MM (line 3490), StoreDwell (line 4802), and CheckThrottle_AtFullThrottle (line 6814).

  • Governs the selection between DLYTIM (moving) and DLYTIM2 (stopped).

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Rolling Idle Surge / Stall: If pushing the clutch in while rolling up to a red light at 10-15 MPH causes the engine to dip and stall, or conversely if the engine hangs at 1,500 RPM until the vehicle comes to a dead stop, MPHREF defines the boundary.
  • Tuning Recommendations: Lowering MPHREF to 3-4 MPH forces the ECU into stationary idle control earlier. Raising MPHREF to 8-10 MPH extends deceleration dashpot protection longer during low-speed rolling traffic.

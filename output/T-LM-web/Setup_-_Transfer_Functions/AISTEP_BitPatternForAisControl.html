1. Overview & Purpose

AISTEP (BitPatternForAisControl) is the hardware commutation table (4 bytes: 0x06, 0x0A, 0x09, 0x05) that defines the sequential magnetic coil excitation patterns for the Automatic Idle Speed bipolar stepper motor.

The Chrysler AIS actuator contains two independent stator windings. To step the rotor forward (opening the pintle) or backward (closing the pintle), the ECU's quad-driver output transistors must energize the coil pairs in a precise quadrature sequence: - Phase 0 (0x06, binary 00000110): Coils A+ and B+ energized. - Phase 1 (0x0A, binary 00001010): Coils A- and B+ energized. - Phase 2 (0x09, binary 00001001): Coils A- and B- energized. - Phase 3 (0x05, binary 00000101): Coils A+ and B- energized.

Inside Operate_AIS_Motor, the ECU increments (+1) or decrements (-1) AISMotorBits, masks to 2 bits (andb #0x03), and fetches the coil bit pattern from AISTEP to drive the motor hardware.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Evaluated inside subroutine Operate_AIS_Motor at line 5402 and initialization in ResetRoutine at line 5644.
  • Indexed by 2-bit phase pointer AISMotorBits (0 to 3).
  • Outputs directly to the ECU's power output driver latch.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Fixed Hardware Driver Table: NEVER MODIFY. Changing these bit patterns will cause the stepper motor coils to fight each other, resulting in a buzzing, vibrating motor that cannot rotate, leading to permanent idle motor failure or blown driver transistors in the Power Module / SMEC.

1. Overview & Purpose

MTHRMX (ValueOf_MINTHR_WhenBatteryDisconnected) is the default baseline closed-throttle voltage loaded into MINTHR_LowestSessionTPS whenever the battery is disconnected or the ECU undergoes a hard power reset.

Factory TPS sensors typically output 0.6V to 0.9V at closed throttle, but vary due to mechanical mounting tolerances and throttle shaft wear. Rather than requiring manual potentiometer calibration with a voltmeter, Chrysler implemented an auto-zeroing adaptive TPS baseline: 1. Upon battery reconnect, MINTHR is initialized to MTHRMX (0x5C / ~1.8V). 2. The very first time the driver starts the car and leaves their foot off the gas, the live TPS reading will be lower than MTHRMX. 3. The ECU ratchets MINTHR downward until it locks onto the true mechanical stop voltage. 4. MTHRMX also acts as a hard sanity ceiling; the ECU will never learn a closed-throttle baseline higher than MTHRMX.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Loaded during power-on initialization in ResetRoutine at line 5614 and line 5727.
  • Evaluated as the upper ceiling clamp in CheckThrottle_AtFullThrottle at lines 6759, 6836, and 6838.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Aftermarket Throttle Bodies (Holley / Ford 5.0 / Willys): When retrofitting aftermarket throttle bodies, the closed-throttle sensor voltage might rest higher or lower than stock. Ensure the closed-throttle output voltage is strictly below 1.5V (well below MTHRMX of 1.8V), so the auto-zeroing routine can latch onto the physical stop.
  • Erratic Idle After Battery Disconnect: When first reconnecting the battery, always turn the key ON and wait 2-3 seconds without touching the gas pedal to allow MINTHR to ratchet down to the mechanical stop.

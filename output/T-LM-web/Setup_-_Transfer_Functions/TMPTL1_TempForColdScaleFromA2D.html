1. Overview & Purpose

TMPTL1 (TempForColdScaleFromA2D) is the primary non-linear analog transfer lookup table that translates raw 8-bit ADC voltages from the Engine Coolant Temperature (ECT) sensor into engineering temperature units when operating on the cold temperature range.

Automotive coolant thermistors have severe exponential resistance curves (hundreds of thousands of ohms when freezing, dropping to a few hundred ohms when boiling). An 8-bit ADC measuring a single resistor divider would lose all resolution at either cold or hot extremes. Chrysler solved this by creating a switched dual-bias circuit: - On cold engines, the ECU engages a high-range bias resistor on Port 1 (bitb #0x08) and references TMPTL1. - When the engine warms up past the crossover point, the ECU toggles the bias resistor (ToggleCTSbias) and transitions to TMPTL2. This provides exceptional ± 1°F measurement precision across the entire span from -40°F to +260°F.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Evaluated inside subroutine GetCoolantTempQuick at line 950 and ResetRoutine at line 5743.
  • Active when Port 1 bias bit is set (BitFlags_Port1 bit 3 = 1).

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Sensor Swaps / Custom Sensors: If swapping to an aftermarket or GM coolant temperature sensor with a different thermistor resistance curve, TMPTL1 and TMPTL2 must be recalibrated to match the new sensor's voltage output.
  • Factory Sensor: Leave stock for factory Chrysler 2-pin coolant sensors.

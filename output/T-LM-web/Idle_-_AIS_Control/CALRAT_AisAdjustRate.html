1. Overview & Purpose

CALRAT (AisAdjustRate) is a timing bitmask applied against the ECU's master loop counter (Counter_MainLoop1) that governs how rapidly the AIS stepper motor is permitted to step downward (close) when returning to target idle.

Stepper motors have mechanical inertia and physical response times. If the ECU decremented the stepper motor pintle every single 11-millisecond program loop when engine speed is above target, the valve would close too rapidly, causing the engine to undershoot target idle and stumble. CALRAT forces the downward step decrement logic to only execute periodically (governed by the bitmask test), creating a smooth, stable, damped return to curb idle without RPM hunting or oscillating.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Evaluated in CalculateTargetIdleSpeedAndAisPosition_MM at line 5292.
  • Active when desired AIS position is greater than target position (cmpa DesiredNewAisPosition; bcc LE65B).
  • Tested via bitwise AND: bitb CALRAT_AisAdjustRate. If bits match, downward decrement is inhibited for that loop pass.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Permissible Values: Must be power-of-two minus one bitmasks or specific bit combinations (0x01, 0x03, 0x07, 0x0E, 0x0F, 0x1F, 0x3F). Stock calibration uses 0x0E.
  • Faster Idle Return: Lowering the mask density allows more frequent decrements, making the idle drop faster after revving the engine. However, setting this too fast will induce RPM undershoot and stalling.
  • Solving RPM Dip / Stall on Decel: If the engine surges downward and almost stalls when returning to idle, slowing the decrement rate (higher bitmask or higher bit count) prevents the AIS from closing faster than the engine airflow can stabilize.

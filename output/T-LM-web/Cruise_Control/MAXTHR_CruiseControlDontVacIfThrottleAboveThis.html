1. Overview & Purpose

MAXTHR (CruiseControlDontVacIfThrottleAboveThis) establishes the maximum throttle angle ceiling allowed under cruise control operation.

The factory vacuum cruise actuator is designed for modest cruising throttle adjustments. If the vehicle encounters a severe hill or load requiring more than ~30-35% throttle, pulling full vacuum to wide open throttle could cause uncontrollable runaway acceleration if a mechanical vacuum valve were to stick. Furthermore, operating a turbocharged engine at high boost under automated cruise control could result in dangerous engine conditions without driver awareness. MAXTHR places an absolute electronic clamp on vacuum pull; once throttle position reaches MAXTHR, no additional vacuum is commanded.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Evaluated inside subroutine CruiseControlSet at line 2368.
  • Checked during active cruise vacuum pull commands.
  • If Throttle_Position >= MAXTHR, vacuum solenoid pulsing is stopped.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Steep Mountain Grades: In hilly terrain, if the car loses speed on steep grades because cruise stops pulling at ~30% throttle, tuners can raise MAXTHR to 0x60 or 0x70 to give the cruise system more authority to hold speed.
  • Safety Margin: Do not raise MAXTHR all the way to 100% (WOT). Maintaining a reasonable ceiling prevents runaway conditions and avoids entering high boost under cruise control.

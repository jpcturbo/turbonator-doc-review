1. Overview & Purpose

TTEMPL defines the minimum engine coolant temperature required at engine startup to arm the thermostat warm-up diagnostic monitor.

The engine controller monitors how quickly the engine warms up to operating temperature to detect a stuck-open thermostat (Fault Code 17). However, during severe sub-freezing arctic cold starts, engine oil and coolant are extremely cold, and the engine takes substantially longer to warm up even with a perfectly functioning thermostat.

In factory calibrations, TTEMPL is set to 99 counts, corresponding to approximately 40 deg F. If coolant temperature at key-on is colder than TTEMPL, the controller suspends arming of the thermostat diagnostic timer, preventing false fault codes in sub-freezing winter weather.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Winter Climate Lockout:
  • Factory setting of 40 deg F disables the thermostat warm-up test during freezing starts, preventing false stuck-open codes.
  • High-Efficiency Radiators:
  • If an aftermarket aluminum radiator prolongs warm-up in cool weather, raise TTEMPL to 55-60 deg F.
  • Edge Cases:
  • Setting TTEMPL to zero forces the warm-up test on freezing starts, reliably triggering Fault Code 17 in winter.
  • Setting TTEMPL higher than TTEMPH permanently disables thermostat diagnostics.

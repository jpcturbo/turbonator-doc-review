1. Overview & Purpose

ERRTMP specifies the minimum engine coolant temperature required before the engine controller enables major system fault escalation and check engine lamp illumination.

During cold engine warm-up, air-fuel mixtures are enriched, combustion stability is lower, and emissions systems have not reached operating temperature. To avoid setting premature trouble codes or alarming the driver during routine cold starts, the engine controller suppresses major fault confirmation until the engine reaches ERRTMP.

In factory calibrations, ERRTMP is set to 160 counts, corresponding to approximately 140 deg F. If coolant temperature is below ERRTMP, the controller bypasses major fault setting, allowing the engine to warm up smoothly before enforcing strict diagnostic monitoring.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cold Climate & Low-Temp Thermostat Tuning:
  • If running a 160 deg F thermostat in cold winter weather, lower ERRTMP from 140 deg F down to 125-130 deg F to ensure major fault diagnostics engage.
  • Warm-Up Suppression:
  • Prevents cold-start fuel enrichment and cold-engine misfire blips from setting latched trouble codes.
  • Edge Cases:
  • Setting ERRTMP to zero enables major fault escalation on freezing cold starts.
  • Setting ERRTMP to maximum (255 counts) permanently disables major error code latching.

1. Overview & Purpose

CHGLOW establishes the lower electrical voltage boundary for the charge air temperature sensor circuit.

The charge air temperature sensor measures compressed intake air in the intake manifold using a negative temperature coefficient thermistor. Because thermistor resistance decreases as charge air warms, signal voltage drops closer to ground at high temperatures. If the signal wire chafes and shorts directly to engine ground or if the thermistor fails internally, signal voltage drops toward zero.

In factory calibrations, CHGLOW is set to 8 counts, corresponding to approximately 0.16 volts. When the sensor signal falls below CHGLOW, the engine controller flags Fault Code 23, latches a charge temperature sensor error, and substitutes default temperature DEFCHG.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • High-Boost & Non-Intercooled Applications:
  • High boost pressures generate elevated charge temperatures that pull sensor voltage low.
  • Ensure CHGLOW remains at or below 8 counts (0.16 volts) to prevent false Code 23 triggers under high-boost dyno or track operation.
  • Short Circuit Troubleshooting:
  • Inspect the two-pin charge temperature harness near the intake manifold runners for chafing against metal brackets.
  • Lowering & Edge Cases:
  • Lowering CHGLOW to zero disables short-circuit fault logging.
  • Raising CHGLOW too high causes false diagnostic fault codes during normal hot engine operation.

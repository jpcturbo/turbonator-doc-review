1. Overview & Purpose

BAROHI defines the maximum credible atmospheric pressure threshold used during barometric pressure validation.

When the engine controller samples barometric pressure at key-on or during closed-throttle deceleration, it evaluates the measured reading against BAROHI to verify sensor plausibility. Standard sea level atmospheric pressure is approximately 14.7 psi absolute (29.9 inHg), and extreme meteorological high-pressure weather cells rarely exceed 15.2 psi absolute. In factory calibrations, BAROHI is configured to 133 counts, corresponding to approximately 15.3 psi absolute (31.1 inHg). If an atmospheric reading equals or exceeds BAROHI, the controller identifies the measurement as electrically implausible (such as a 5-volt reference short or broken sensor ground) and substitutes the failsafe default value BARDFT.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • 3-Bar MAP Sensor Rescaling:
  • On a 3-bar sensor, rescale BAROHI from factory 133 counts down to ~88 counts (15.2 psi on 44.1 psi scale).
  • Failsafe Substitution:
  • Any reading meeting or exceeding BAROHI is immediately rejected, and BARDFT is loaded into memory.
  • Diagnostic Protection:
  • Protects fuel calibration against high-voltage sensor wire shorts and disconnected sensor ground circuits.

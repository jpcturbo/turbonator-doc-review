1. Overview & Purpose

FANMAP defines the turbocharger boost pressure threshold relative to barometric pressure that turns on the radiator and intercooler cooling fan.

In factory turbocharged vehicles where the radiator and intercooler share the forward front-end airflow duct, sustained turbo boost generates localized charge air heating and underhood thermal stress. When manifold pressure exceeds barometric pressure by FANMAP, the engine controller energizes the cooling fan regardless of coolant temperature, drawing extra airflow through the intercooler core. In factory 2.5L ATX calibrations, FANMAP is configured to 255 counts (29.4 psi boost), effectively disabling this boost-activated fan feature for conservative commuter driving.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Intercooler Heat Soak Prevention:
  • Lower FANMAP to 2.0 to 4.0 psi boost (17 to 35 counts) to spool the fan upon turbo boost onset.
  • 3-Bar MAP Scaling:
  • Rescale FANMAP for 3-bar conversions to account for the wider 0 to 44.1 psi absolute sensor calibration.
  • Disabling Boost-Triggered Fan:
  • Set FANMAP to 255 counts (factory setting) to prevent boost from activating the radiator fan.

5. Calibration Notes & System Dependencies

  • Hysteresis Deadband: Firmware automatically adds 2 counts (~0.23 psi on 2-bar) of hysteresis when the fan is already active.
  • 3-Bar MAP Scaling: On a 3-bar sensor, 255 counts represents 44.1 psi absolute; a 2.0 psi boost threshold requires ~12 counts.
  • Overflow Protection: If BaroPressure + FANMAP overflows beyond 255 counts, carry is set and the logic safely defaults to turning the fan off.

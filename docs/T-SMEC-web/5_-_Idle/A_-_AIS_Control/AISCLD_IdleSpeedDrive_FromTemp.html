1. Overview & Purpose

AISCLD defines the target idle speed curve across engine coolant temperatures when an automatic transmission is engaged in gear (Drive, Reverse, 1st, or 2nd).

When an automatic transmission is shifted into gear, the torque converter impeller creates substantial viscous drag against the engine crankshaft. To prevent the vehicle from excessively creeping forward against the brakes while stopped at traffic lights, and to reduce transmission fluid heating, the controller targets a lower idle speed in gear than in neutral.

AISCLD is looked up by coolant temperature whenever the in-gear flag is active.

In factory calibrations, AISCLD commands approximately 1200 RPM during cold warm-up, tapering down to a hot in-gear target of 800 to 850 RPM once fully warmed up.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • In-Gear Camshaft Stall Prevention:
  • Raise warm in-gear targets from 800 RPM to 875 to 950 RPM if an aggressive camshaft pulls down vacuum and threatens to stall against the torque converter.
  • High-Stall Torque Converters:
  • High-stall aftermarket converters create less idle drag; in-gear target can be adjusted closer to Park/Neutral target without causing harsh transmission engagement.
  • Relative Offset Rule:
  • Keep AISCLD 50 to 100 RPM lower than AISPN across all temperature points to prevent engine flare when shifting out of gear.

1. Overview & Purpose

DAISOP is the primary 2D calibration curve that determines how many steps to open the automatic idle speed stepper motor during rapid throttle deceleration.

When the driver suddenly releases the accelerator pedal, intake manifold pressure plunges toward deep vacuum. The SMEC subtracts actual manifold pressure from the calculated decel control manifold pressure to obtain a negative delta MAP value representing the severity of the deceleration vacuum transient.

DAISOP indexes this pressure drop to command instantaneous opening steps on the stepper motor, acting as a high-speed electronic dashpot to cushion the engine's descent and prevent stalling.

In factory calibrations, DAISOP commands 0 opening steps at tiny pressure deltas (below 0.1 psi), ramping up to 11 opening steps as the deceleration vacuum drop approaches 1.4 psi (2.8 inHg).

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Eliminating Decel Clutch-In Stalls:
  • Increase DAISOP opening steps from 11 to 16 to 22 steps if the engine dies when disengaging the clutch during deceleration.
  • Lightweight Flywheel Cushion:
  • Lightened rotational assemblies decelerate rapidly; aggressive DAISOP opening steps arrest the downward RPM plunge.
  • Preventing Decel Flare:
  • If engine speed surges upward or hangs above 1500 RPM when releasing the throttle, reduce DAISOP steps to allow a faster return to idle.

1. Overview & Purpose

REVLMT defines the lower engine speed recovery threshold used to restore fuel injection when the engine controller is operating in MAP sensor limp-in mode with the throttle opened beyond 25 percent.

If the manifold absolute pressure sensor fails due to an open circuit, short to ground, or stuck signal, the controller cannot directly measure engine load and must synthesize an estimated pressure value from throttle position and engine speed. To prevent runaway engine speeds and high-load detonation under synthetic fueling, the controller restricts maximum engine speed. When engine speed exceeds REVLMT2 (approximately 2000 RPM), fuel injection is cut. REVLMT establishes the lower hysteresis setpoint (58 counts, or approximately 1850 RPM), allowing fuel to re-engage once the engine slows down.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Hysteresis Relationship:
  • Paired with REVLMT2 (upper cutout threshold). Always keep REVLMT 100 to 200 RPM below REVLMT2 to avoid rapid fuel cut oscillation.
  • Limp-Home Speed Adjustment:
  • Raising REVLMT and REVLMT2 allows higher road speed when limping a vehicle with a disconnected or failed MAP sensor.
  • Inversion Risk:
  • Never set REVLMT above REVLMT2, as doing so prevents fuel restoration once the rev limiter trips.

1. Overview & Purpose

DECEL defines the coolant temperature-dependent fuel multiplier applied when manifold absolute pressure drops rapidly during engine deceleration.

As the throttle closes, intake manifold vacuum deepens sharply, vaporizing raw fuel off the intake runner walls and creating a rich air-fuel mixture. To prevent spark plug fouling, unburnt hydrocarbon emissions, and exhaust burble, the engine controller looks up DECEL when manifold pressure drops exceed the second trigger threshold.

The factory calibration applies no fuel reduction at coolant temperatures below 144 degF to protect cold driveability, and ramps up to an 7.4 percent fuel reduction once the engine warms to 177 degF.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Adjusting Deceleration Leaning: Increasing table values (closer to 255 counts, or 1.00 factor) reduces the amount of deceleration fuel leaning, preserving richer mixtures during lift-off.
  • Fuel Economy and Engine Braking: Lowering table values cuts fuel more aggressively, improving fuel economy and engine braking on long downhill descents.
  • Exhaust Crackle and Pop Calibration: Tuners seeking exhaust crackle, burble, or pops on deceleration often zero out or disable this fuel reduction table while adjusting ignition timing.
  • Re-Acceleration Transition: Cutting too much fuel on turbocharged engines can cause lean hesitations upon re-acceleration if manifold wall-wetting is depleted.

  • Primary Deceleration Multiplier: Acts as the principal map-based decel fuel leaning parameter in SMEC firmware.

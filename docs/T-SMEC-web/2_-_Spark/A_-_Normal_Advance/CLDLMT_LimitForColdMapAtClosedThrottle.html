1. Overview & Purpose

CLDLMT establishes the maximum allowable ignition advance ceiling that the cold manifold pressure spark curve (CLDMAP) can contribute while the engine is at closed throttle.

During cold start-up and warm-up idle, manifold vacuum can fluctuate significantly due to high idle air bypass, cold mechanical friction, and temperature transitions. If the cold MAP curve were allowed to command unrestricted advance during closed-throttle coasting or idle, the engine could suffer from high idle speed flaring, erratic surging, or delayed deceleration recovery. In factory calibrations, CLDLMT is calibrated to 30 counts, enforcing a hard upper ceiling of 15.0 degrees BTDC on the cold MAP timing contribution when the throttle is closed.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cold Idle Stabilization:
  • Raising CLDLMT increases the closed-throttle cold timing cap, providing additional torque to support cold idle on engines with large camshafts or heavy accessory loads.
  • Preventing Idle Hang & Surging:
  • Setting CLDLMT too high allows excessive timing advance under deep overrun vacuum, causing cold idle speed to hang or surge.
  • Lowering & Deceleration Recovery:
  • Lowering CLDLMT ensures a stable, controlled deceleration drop, but setting it too low can cause the engine to undershoot target idle and stumble upon throttle release.

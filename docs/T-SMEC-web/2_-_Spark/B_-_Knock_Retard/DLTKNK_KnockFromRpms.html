1. Overview & Purpose

DLTKNK defines the knock sensor acoustic noise threshold curve indexed by engine speed (RPM).

A piezoelectric knock sensor mounted to the engine cylinder block converts structural vibrations into an AC electrical voltage, which the engine controller rectifies and samples. Normal mechanical engine noise—caused by valvetrain harmonics, piston skirt motion, connecting rod bearing reversals, and accessory drives—naturally amplifies as engine speed increases. To prevent normal mechanical noise from falsely triggering knock retard at high RPM, DLTKNK establishes the baseline acoustic threshold: any knock sensor voltage reading exceeding the curve at that RPM is flagged as genuine combustion detonation.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Filtering Valvetrain & Piston Noise:
  • Raising DLTKNK in the 3000 to 5500 RPM band eliminates false knock retard caused by forged piston slap or stiff valve springs.
  • Preserving Knock Protection:
  • Never raise DLTKNK to maximum voltage (5.0V) across the board; doing so completely disables knock detection, leaving the engine unprotected against bad fuel or overboost.
  • Datalogging & Calibration:
  • Monitor raw knock sensor voltage during dyno pulls; calibrate DLTKNK roughly 0.3 to 0.5 Volts above the engine's baseline mechanical noise floor to catch genuine detonation spikes.

1. Overview & Purpose

DWELL defines the primary ignition coil dwell time curve indexed by battery voltage.

Ignition coil dwell represents the duration in microseconds that the ignition driver transistor grounds the coil primary winding, allowing electrical current and magnetic flux to saturate the coil core before opening the circuit to fire the spark plug. Battery voltage is the primary physical factor governing coil saturation: at low battery voltages (such as during cold cranking or charging system faults, between 8 and 10 Volts), current builds slowly through the coil windings, requiring extended dwell times (up to 8.2 milliseconds). At normal vehicle operating voltages (13.5 to 14.5 Volts), the coil charges much faster, requiring only 2.2 to 2.5 milliseconds. The calibration table stores the mathematical one's complement of the dwell period because the controller's 16-bit table interpolation routine requires an upward-sloping curve.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Aftermarket Coil Matching:
  • High-performance aftermarket coils (such as MSD Blaster or Accel coils) typically have lower primary resistance; consult coil manufacturer specifications to avoid over-saturating the primary winding.
  • Preventing Spark Blowout:
  • On high-boost applications (20+ psi), slightly increasing dwell at 13 to 14 Volts ensures full coil saturation to jump large spark plug gaps under dense cylinder pressures.
  • Coil Overheating Caution:
  • Never set dwell beyond coil saturation; excessive dwell dissipates purely as heat inside the coil casing and power transistor.
  • One's Complement Encoding:
  • Stored in ROM as the one's complement; MPTune handles the inversion automatically so the tuner views true microseconds.

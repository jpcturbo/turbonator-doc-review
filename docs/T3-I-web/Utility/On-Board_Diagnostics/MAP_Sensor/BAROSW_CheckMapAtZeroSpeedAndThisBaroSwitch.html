1. Overview & Purpose

BAROSW defines the barometric pressure switch point used to select between sea-level and high-altitude intake manifold vacuum criteria during the idle pneumatic MAP sensor check. Because ambient atmospheric pressure decreases significantly with increasing altitude, an engine idling at high elevation naturally produces less absolute manifold vacuum than the same engine idling at sea level. To prevent vehicles operating in mountain regions from falsely setting Fault Code 13, the engine controller evaluates atmospheric barometric pressure at key-on and chooses an appropriate vacuum test threshold. In factory calibrations, BAROSW is set to 98 counts, corresponding to approximately 11.3 psi barometric pressure (equivalent to an altitude of roughly 7500 feet above sea level). When atmospheric pressure is below BAROSW, the controller switches to a relaxed high-altitude vacuum check threshold.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • 3-Bar MAP Sensor Rescaling: * Multiply BAROSW by 0.667 (from 98 counts down to 65 counts) when converting from a 2-bar to a 3-bar MAP sensor. * This maintains the physical 11.3 psi (7500 ft elevation) transition point under the 3-bar sensor transfer function.
  • High Altitude Tuning: * For vehicles operating primarily in high-altitude mountain environments, raising BAROSW slightly ensures early engagement of relaxed vacuum thresholds.
  • Edge Cases: * Setting BAROSW to 255 counts forces high-altitude vacuum criteria at all elevations. * Setting BAROSW to zero forces sea-level criteria even at the highest mountain passes.

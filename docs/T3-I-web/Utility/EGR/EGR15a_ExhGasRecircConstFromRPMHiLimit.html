1. Overview & Purpose

EGR15A (ExhGasRecircConstFromRPMHiLimit15a) represents the low byte of the 16-bit Phase 2 diagnostic timeout timer threshold in the Exhaust Gas Recirculation verification routine. Although labeled in calibration templates as an engine speed high limit, firmware reverse engineering of T-T3v1_v19.asm confirms that this label is a complete misnomer. The actual RPM limits are governed by EGR15/EGR15B (Phase 1) and EGR17/EGR17A (Phase 2). In executable firmware, EGR15A resides in calibration memory directly following EGR6. When the CPU executes line 5485, it reads a 16-bit word where EGR6 is the high byte (1 count) and EGR15A is the low byte (108 counts), combining into the 364-count diagnostic timeout threshold.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Template Misnomer Anomaly: Labeled in MPTune as ExhGasRecircConstFromRPMHiLimit, but has no connection to RPM. It is the low byte of 16-bit constant EGR6.
  • Fine-Tuning Timeout: Each count added or subtracted adjusts the Phase 2 timeout window by approximately 10 to 15 milliseconds.
  • Combined Math: Total Phase 2 timeout counts equal (EGR6 * 256) + EGR15A. Factory settings: (1 * 256) + 108 = 364 counts (approx 3.6 to 5.5 seconds).
  • Diagnostic Tuning: If installing headers causes delayed oxygen sensor response, raising EGR15A to 200 or raising EGR6 to 2 provides extra response headroom.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during warm, part-throttle cruising, modulating the exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) solenoid duty cycle to control combustion temperatures.

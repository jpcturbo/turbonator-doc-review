1. Overview & Purpose

TMPSWP defines the coolant temperature switch threshold that divides the engine controller's ignition advance logic between Cold Engine Mode and Warm Engine Mode. Cold engines have cold cylinder walls, reduced fuel vaporization, and slower combustion speeds, requiring the dedicated cold timing curve (CLDMAP) to maintain smooth operation. Once the engine coolant warms past TMPSWP, the controller transitions to its primary warm ignition advance curves (HOTMAP and WOTMAP). In factory calibrations, TMPSWP is configured to 146 counts, corresponding to approximately 147 degF, aligning with the engine warm-up curve as the thermostat begins to regulate coolant circulation.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Thermostat Matching: * When installing a cooler 160 degF thermostat, lower TMPSWP slightly (e.g., to 135-140 counts / ~130-135 degF) to ensure consistent entry into warm timing mode under cool ambient weather.
  • Extending Cold Timing for Large Cams: * Raising TMPSWP keeps the engine on CLDMAP longer during warmup, which can stabilize cold drive-away on engines with aggressive camshaft profiles.
  • Lowering & Premature Warm Mode: * Setting TMPSWP excessively low causes the controller to switch to leaner, less aggressive warm timing before the combustion chambers are warm, resulting in cold flat spots or hesitation.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect once the engine coolant warms past TMPSWP, the controller transitions to its primary warm ignition advance curves (HOTMAP and WOTMAP).

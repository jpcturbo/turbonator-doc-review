1. Overview & Purpose

WSGRPM defines the primary wide-open throttle turbocharger boost target curve as a function of engine speed. The engine controller uses this table to establish the baseline gauge boost ceiling across the entire operating rev range. Factory calibrations typically establish a conservative mid-range boost plateau of approximately 9 to 11 psi of boost above atmospheric pressure, gently tapering the target at high engine speeds to prevent excessive exhaust backpressure and keep the turbocharger within its peak compressor efficiency island. This table acts as the foundational boost ceiling that all secondary safety tables compare against.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Primary Boost Ceiling Adjustment: * Elevating points raises the peak boost target across the RPM range for increased engine power. * Common performance targets range from 14 to 18 psi on factory 2-bar MAP sensors, and 20 to 30 psi on 3-bar sensors.
  • Minimum-Wins Target Hierarchy: * The ECU evaluates six tables sequentially (WSGRPM, WSGTIM, WSGCLT, WSGCHG, MXSBST, WSGPRT) and selects the smallest value. * Companion limit tables must be raised alongside WSGRPM to prevent unexpected boost capping.
  • 3-Bar MAP Sensor Rescaling: * Converting to a 3-bar sensor doubles the pressure span per count, requiring complete table rescale to maintain intended boost targets.
  • Turbine Flow Considerations: * Tapering boost targets slightly above 5200 RPM helps prevent severe exhaust manifold backpressure on small turbine housings.

1. Overview & Purpose

RICHSA establishes the open-loop fuel enrichment factor injected whenever the engine controller enters mechanical basic ignition timing service mode. To properly synchronize distributor orientation with true engine top dead center using a timing light, the technician disconnects the coolant temperature sensor, forcing a coolant sensor diagnostic fault. In response, the engine controller locks ignition advance to a fixed reference angle, commands a steady idle airflow, and loads RICHSA as a rich open-loop fueling multiplier. In factory calibrations, RICHSA is set to 26 counts. This provides extra fuel dampening to stabilize combustion, prevent lean misfiring, and suppress engine speed oscillations while closed-loop oxygen sensor feedback and adaptive fuel memory updates are temporarily suspended.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Factory Baseline & Retention: * Retain RICHSA at its factory value of 26 counts for standard street engines to maintain predictable open-loop fueling during distributor synchronization.
  • Aftermarket Camshafts & Overlap: * Raising RICHSA slightly (up to 30 to 35 counts) helps stabilize erratic combustion on aggressive camshafts that generate poor idle manifold vacuum.
  • Large Injector Considerations: * When installing large aftermarket fuel injectors, ensure overall fuel scaling is trimmed so that the uncorrected multiplier in RICHSA does not saturate cylinders or foul spark plugs during timing light alignment.
  • Lowering & Lean Stumbles: * Lowering RICHSA below 20 counts can produce lean idle instability, causing the engine to surge or die when entering diagnostic basic timing mode.

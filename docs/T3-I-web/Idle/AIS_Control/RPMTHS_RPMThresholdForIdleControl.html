1. Overview & Purpose

RPMTHS defines the engine speed threshold separating starter motor cranking or engine stall states from active engine running idle control. While the engine is being cranked by the starter or if the engine stalls and RPM drops precipitously, normal closed-loop idle speed regulation must be suspended to prevent erratic stepper motor oscillations. Whenever actual engine speed is below RPMTHS, the engine controller bypasses normal closed-loop idle routines and forces the synthetic target speed defined by RPMDEF. In factory calibrations, RPMTHS is set to 25 counts, representing an engine speed threshold of 200 RPM. Once crankshaft rotation exceeds 200 RPM, the controller recognizes that combustion has initiated and begins transitioning toward normal closed-loop idle air control.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cranking vs Running Boundary: * Keep RPMTHS at 200 RPM (25 counts) to maintain an unambiguous separation between starter cranking and autonomous engine idle.
  • Low Setting Danger: * Lowering RPMTHS below starter cranking speed causes premature engagement of closed-loop idle feedback, inducing stepper motor hunting during cranking.
  • High Setting Delay: * Setting RPMTHS too high traps the controller in startup mode after firing, delaying normal closed-loop idle regulation.

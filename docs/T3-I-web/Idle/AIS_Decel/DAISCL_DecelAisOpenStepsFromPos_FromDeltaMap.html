1. Overview & Purpose

DAISCL is the 2D calibration curve that determines how many steps to close the automatic idle speed stepper motor as intake manifold pressure recovers during deceleration. Following an initial deceleration throttle snap-shut where DAISOP opened the stepper motor to catch engine speed, intake manifold pressure begins to normalize toward steady-state idle vacuum. If the idle air motor remained in its wide-open dashpot position once engine speed approached idle, the engine would hang at elevated RPM or race uncontrollably. When actual manifold pressure rises above the calculated decel control manifold pressure, DAISCL looks up closing steps (negative values) to retract the stepper motor back toward its calibrated idle position. In factory calibrations, DAISCL commands 0 closing steps at small pressure deltas, ramping up to -5 steps as positive delta manifold pressure reaches 2.0 psi (4.1 inHg).

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Curing Deceleration Idle Hang: * Increase negative closing step magnitude in DAISCL (from -5 up to -8 or -10 steps) if engine speed lingers above 1300 RPM after releasing the accelerator.
  • Preventing Landing Undershoot: * If the engine catches on decel but then dives below idle as it settles, reduce DAISCL closing steps to create a smoother, slower air retraction.
  • Dashpot Equilibrium: * Balance DAISCL against DAISOP to ensure a smooth transition from deceleration dashpot into steady closed-throttle idle.

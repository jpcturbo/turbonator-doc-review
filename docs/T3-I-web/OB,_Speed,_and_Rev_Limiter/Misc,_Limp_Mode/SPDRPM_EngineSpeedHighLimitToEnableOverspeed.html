1. Overview & Purpose

SPDRPM defines the engine speed arming threshold that gates the vehicle road speed limiter. In Chrysler engine management architecture, the vehicle speed limiter (SPDLMH) is not continuously active across all operating conditions; rather, it is only evaluated when engine speed is at or above SPDRPM. In factory calibrations, SPDRPM is set to 125 counts, corresponding to 4000 RPM. If the vehicle is traveling at high speed in neutral, coasting down a mountain grade, or driving in top gear below 4000 RPM, the vehicle speed fuel cut will not trigger. This prevents unexpected engine fuel cutouts during low-RPM highway cruising.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Speed Limiter Arming Ceiling: * Raising SPDRPM to 5500+ RPM delays vehicle speed limit evaluation until top-end RPM is reached.
  • Tall Final-Drive Ratios: * On vehicles with tall overdrive ratios that cruise at high MPH with low RPM, ensure SPDRPM is set appropriately if speed governing is desired.
  • Disabling Alternative: * Setting SPDLMH to 255 counts is the standard method for disabling top speed cut, making SPDRPM inactive in practice.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect at elevated engine RPM as a prerequisite check, ensuring the vehicle speed limiter is only armed and evaluated when engine speed is above this threshold.

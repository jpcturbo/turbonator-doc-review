1. Overview & Purpose

DFRPM1 defines the engine speed recovery threshold that restores fuel injection following deceleration fuel shutoff initiated through the high-RPM over-rev decel pathway. When a vehicle decelerates from high engine speed with the throttle closed, the controller cuts fuel injection to improve fuel economy, lower exhaust emissions, and provide engine braking. As the vehicle slows and engine speed falls toward idle, fuel must be restored before the engine reaches idle speed to prevent stalling or rough engine sag. In factory calibrations, DFRPM1 is set to 64 counts, representing approximately 2050 RPM. If deceleration fuel cut was latched via Turbonator_OverRev, the controller maintains fuel shutoff until engine speed decelerates below DFRPM1, at which point fuel injection resumes smoothly.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Exhaust Pop & Stall Prevention: * Raising DFRPM1 to 2400 to 2600 RPM restores fuel earlier, preventing exhaust backfire and stalling on lightweight flywheels.
  • Engine Braking & Economy: * Lowering DFRPM1 toward 1600 RPM extends fuel cut duration during coast-down, enhancing engine braking.
  • Idle Transition Stability: * Keep DFRPM1 safely above target idle speed to prevent engine sag when returning to idle.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during closed-throttle deceleration from high engine speed, restoring fuel injection once engine RPM drops below approximately 2,050 RPM to prevent engine stalling.

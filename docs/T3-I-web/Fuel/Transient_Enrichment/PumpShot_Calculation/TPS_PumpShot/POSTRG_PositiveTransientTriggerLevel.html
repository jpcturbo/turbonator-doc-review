1. Overview & Purpose

POSTRG defines the minimum rate of throttle position sensor voltage increase required to trigger a throttle-based acceleration enrichment pump shot. When the driver presses the accelerator pedal, the throttle blade opens before the intake manifold plenum can fill with air and register an increased pressure reading. To prevent an immediate lean hesitation or stumble, the engine controller calculates the difference between instantaneous throttle voltage and a rolling average throttle voltage. If this delta exceeds POSTRG, the controller activates acceleration pump fueling. The factory setting of 3 counts represents approximately 0.06 volts, filtering out minor driver foot vibrations, electrical noise, and road bumps while ensuring immediate response to deliberate pedal inputs.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Tip-in Sensitivity: Lowering POSTRG makes the throttle acceleration pump more sensitive, firing extra fuel on very small throttle inputs to eliminate hairline tip-in hesitations.
  • Preventing False Triggers: Setting this value to zero or near zero can cause false pump shots from throttle shaft vibration or road roughness, degrading fuel economy and causing subtle rich surges during highway cruising.
  • Large Throttle Bodies: Raising POSTRG requires a firmer pedal depression before enrichment activates, which may be beneficial with oversized aftermarket throttle bodies where small angular openings flow significantly more air.
  • Coordination with POSSLP: Always adjust POSTRG in coordination with the gain slope in POSSLP.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during rapid throttle opening (tip-in), triggering acceleration enrichment pump shot fueling when throttle delta exceeds this threshold.

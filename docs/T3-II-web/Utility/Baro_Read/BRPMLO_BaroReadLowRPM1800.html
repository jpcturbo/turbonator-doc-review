1. Overview & Purpose

BRPMLO defines the lower engine speed boundary for in-flight atmospheric barometric pressure sampling during vehicle deceleration. Because turbocharged Chrysler engine controllers utilize a single manifold absolute pressure sensor to monitor both engine manifold pressure and ambient barometric pressure, the controller must periodically energize a baro read solenoid to vent the sensor to underhood atmosphere during closed-throttle deceleration. BRPMLO establishes the minimum engine speed required to permit this update. In factory calibrations, BRPMLO is configured to 1800 RPM. Below 1800 RPM, engine deceleration vacuum diminishes and the engine nears idle transition, which introduces pressure instability that would corrupt atmospheric measurements.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Aftermarket Camshaft Adjustments: * If camshaft lope or weak low-RPM decel vacuum distorts baro readings, raise BRPMLO toward 2000 to 2200 RPM.
  • Idle Transition Buffer: * Avoid setting BRPMLO below 1400 RPM to prevent venting the pressure sensor during dashpot idle capture.
  • Elevation Tracking Stability: * Keeping BRPMLO near 1800 RPM ensures consistent in-flight elevation tracking during highway descents.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during in-flight barometric pressure reads, establishing the lower engine RPM boundary required to cycle the baro solenoid.

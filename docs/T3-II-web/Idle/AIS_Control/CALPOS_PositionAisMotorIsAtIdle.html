1. Overview & Purpose

CALPOS defines the calibrated nominal step position where the electronic controller assumes the idle air stepper motor sits during normal warm closed-throttle idle. Because stepper motors operate without a physical position feedback potentiometer, the ECU tracks motor position by counting step pulses sent to the motor coils. During closed-throttle stationary idle, adaptive routines periodically nudge motor position toward CALPOS to maintain optimal dynamic control range. Additionally, CALPOS serves as the fundamental baseline offset added to startup positioning curves and cold idle tables. In factory calibrations, CALPOS is set to 26 counts, representing 26 open steps from the fully seated pintle position on a stock throttle body.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Aftermarket Throttle Bodies: * Big-bore throttle bodies allow more air leakage; adjust CALPOS down to 10 to 18 steps to match the lower step counts observed in live datalogs.
  • Mechanical Stop Screw Adjustment: * If you turn the base idle stop screw inward to provide more base air for a race camshaft, reduce CALPOS correspondingly to keep adaptive trim centered.
  • Centering Adaptive Trim: * Setting CALPOS accurately gives the idle stepper motor equal dynamic range to step open or closed when loads change.

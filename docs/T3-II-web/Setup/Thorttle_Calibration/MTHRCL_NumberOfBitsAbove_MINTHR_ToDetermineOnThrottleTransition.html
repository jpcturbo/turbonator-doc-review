1. Overview & Purpose

MTHRCL defines the voltage deadband threshold above the learned minimum closed-throttle position required to declare an off-idle throttle transition. In Chrysler engine controllers, maintaining a small deadband above the learned minimum resting throttle voltage prevents engine vibrations, electrical ripple, and minor driver pedal flutter from accidentally knocking the controller out of closed-loop idle control. Factory calibrations configure MTHRCL to two counts, corresponding to approximately zero point zero three nine volts. When accelerator pedal movement raises sensor voltage above the learned minimum plus MTHRCL, the controller sets the off-idle flag, disabling idle air speed motor corrections and engaging acceleration fuel enrichment scheduling.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Tip-In Sensitivity vs Idle Stability: * Increasing MTHRCL to three or four counts (~0.059V to 0.078V) stabilizes idle on cars with stiff engine mounts or rough roads.
  • Hair-Trigger Response: * Lowering MTHRCL to one count (~0.020V) sharpens off-idle throttle pickup but requires pristine sensor grounds.
  • Hysteresis Pair: * Works with MTHROP to create an entry/exit deadband between idle mode and off-idle acceleration mode.
  • Factory Default: * Set to two counts (~0.039 Volts) in factory calibrations.

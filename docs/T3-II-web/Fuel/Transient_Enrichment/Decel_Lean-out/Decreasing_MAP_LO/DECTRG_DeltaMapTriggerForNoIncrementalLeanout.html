1. Overview & Purpose

DECTRG sets the primary manifold pressure drop threshold required to initiate incremental deceleration lean-out. When the vehicle is moving and manifold pressure falls below the rolling average, the engine controller calculates the rate of pressure drop. If this drop exceeds DECTRG, the controller begins incrementing the deceleration lean-out factor by one count on each execution pass. The factory setting of 4 counts corresponds to approximately 0.46 psi of manifold pressure drop, ensuring that gentle cruising throttle adjustments do not trigger unwanted fuel cuts while responding smoothly to moderate deceleration events.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Deceleration Sensitivity: Lowering DECTRG makes MAP deceleration lean-out engage on smaller vacuum increases, helping lean out the mixture sooner during gradual deceleration.
  • Preventing Highway Cruise Surging: Setting this value too low can cause steady-speed cruising surging if manifold vacuum fluctuates slightly on level roads.
  • Camshaft Compatibility: Raising DECTRG prevents false lean-out engagement on camshafts with low manifold vacuum or high pulse amplitude.
  • 3-Bar MAP Calibration: Scale DECTRG down by approximately 33 percent when converting to a 3-bar MAP sensor to preserve the stock 0.46 psi threshold.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during closed-throttle deceleration, establishing the rate-of-throttle-drop trigger level for deceleration leanout.

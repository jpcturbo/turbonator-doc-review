Calibration & Tuning Reference

T-SBEC Engine Modifications Tuning Guide

Chrysler Turbo SBEC (1990–1991) • 1990–1991 consolidated single-board controller (Motorola 68HC11 MCU)

MPTune Automation vs. Tuner Calibration Responsibilities

MPTune provides powerful automated scaling wizards (such as injector pulsewidth rescaling and MAP sensor transfer function generation). However, automated wizards only establish a mathematical baseline. Crucial real-world drivability, transient fueling, injector opening dynamics, and idle stability require focused, manual calibration in specific tables.

This guide provides smart, field-proven calibration guidance for five common performance modifications, identifying exactly which identifiers to modify, by how much, and why, without confusing software jargon.

1. Larger Exhaust & Catalytic Converter Removal (2.5" / 3" Mandrel Systems)

What Happens to the Engine

  • Faster Turbine Pressure Ratio Build: Free-flowing 2.5" or 3" downpipes dramatically reduce turbine discharge backpressure, spooling the turbocharger hundreds of RPM earlier.
  • Boost Overshoot / Spiking: Because the turbo spools much faster than factory exhaust dynamics expect, factory electronic wastegate solenoids can momentarily lag, resulting in transient boost spikes.
  • High-RPM Boost Creep: At high engine speeds (above 5,200 RPM), the small factory internal wastegate port may be physically overwhelmed by huge exhaust volume, causing boost to creep upward.
  • Mechanical Manual Boost Controllers (MBC): Many tuners in this community install mechanical ball-and-spring boost controllers (Grainger valves), completely bypassing ECU wastegate tables. If running an MBC, wastegate duty cycle tables do not affect boost pressure.

Tuner's Field Checklist: Exhaust Upgrades

  1. If retaining factory ECU wastegate solenoid control, lower DCWOT by 10% in the 2,400–3,600 RPM spool region to prevent overshoot spikes.
  2. If running a manual Grainger valve, verify wastegate solenoid vacuum lines are plugged and raise OVBST by +2 psi above target boost to eliminate false fuel cuts.
  3. Inspect downpipe clearance around the steering rack and monitor wideband AFR on full throttle pulls.

2. Bigger Throttle Body (52mm / 58mm vs. Stock 46mm)

What Happens to the Engine

  • Rapid Manifold Vacuum Collapse: Opening a 52mm or 58mm throttle plate admits substantially more volume per degree of throttle rotation than the stock 46mm unit, dropping manifold vacuum instantly on tip-in.
  • Transient Tip-In Stumble (Rich Bog): Factory acceleration enrichment pump shot delivers fuel proportional to throttle angle delta. With a big throttle body, stock pump shot floods the intake runner floor with wet fuel, causing a bog.
  • Elevated Leakage Airflow at Idle: Larger throttle plates leak slightly more perimeter airflow around their circumferences, requiring less idle stepper motor bypass air to hold target RPM.

  1. Mechanically adjust the throttle stop screw with the AIS motor disconnected so warm base idle sits ~750 RPM.
  2. Verify live closed-throttle TPS voltage sits between 0.70V and 0.90V in MPScan, and confirm MINTHR reads baseline voltage.
  3. Raise POSTRG by +20% and lower POSSLP by -20% to eliminate tip-in hesitation.

3. Bigger Injectors (+20%, +40%, 72+ pph) [Fine-Tuning Guidelines]

What Happens to the Engine

  • Ultra-Short Cruising Pulsewidths: Large injectors flow huge fuel volume, dropping idle and part-throttle pulsewidths down toward 1.5ms–2.5ms.
  • Adaptive Learning Lockout: Factory calibration firmware stops updating adaptive fuel trim cells when pulsewidth drops below PWMIN (stock 4.80ms). With large injectors, PWMIN must be scaled down or closed-loop learning halts.
  • Faster Modern Injector Dead Times: Modern disc or multi-hole injectors open much faster than vintage Chrysler pintle injectors, requiring battery latency offset adjustments in BATOFF.
  • Closed-Loop O2 Hunting: Large injectors deliver more fuel per integrator tick, turning subtle closed-loop trims into wild rich/lean RPM surging unless SECRMP is reduced to +1/-1 count.

Tuner's Field Checklist: High-Flow Injectors

  1. Always run MPTune's automated injector scaling wizard first to scale base fuel slope (FSLOPE).
  2. Lower PWMIN to 1.50ms so adaptive fuel trims learn properly during highway cruising.
  3. Reduce SECRMP from +3/-3 down to +1/-1 counts to cure rolling idle hunting in closed-loop mode.
  4. Verify fuel pressure with vacuum hose disconnected sits at factory 55 psi baseline.

4. 3-Bar and 4-Bar MAP Sensors [Fine-Tuning Guidelines]

What Happens to the Engine

  • Compressed Analog Voltage Resolution: A 3-bar sensor spreads 0–5 Volts across 0 to 44 psi absolute (up to ~29 psi boost), halving analog voltage delta per psi compared to a 2-bar sensor.
  • Transfer Slope Scaling: MPTune automatically rescales MAPMULT and MAPADD to convert sensor voltage into correct internal pressure counts.
  • High-Boost Fuel & Spark Cell Tuning: A 3-bar conversion unlocks previously unreachable high-boost lookup cells in fuel and spark tables. These upper cells must be calibrated for safe air-fuel ratios and timing advance.

Tuner's Field Checklist: 3-Bar / 4-Bar Sensors

  1. Use MPTune's MAP Sensor Wizard to convert the calibration template to 3-bar or 4-bar scaling.
  2. Before starting the engine, turn ignition Key-ON and verify live MAP sensor reading in MPScan reads local barometric pressure (~14.7 psi / 29.9 inHg).
  3. Review full-throttle spark timing in WOTMAP in the newly unlocked 15–28 psi cells to ensure conservative advance.

5. Generic Performance Camshaft (Higher Lift, Longer Duration, Tighter LSA)

What Happens to the Engine

  • Lower Idle Vacuum (Higher Absolute Pressure): Long duration and tight lobe separation angles (LSA) cause exhaust gas reversion at idle and low RPM, lowering idle vacuum and causing erratic MAP sensor readings.
  • Shifted Volumetric Efficiency Peak: Performance camshafts breathe much more efficiently above 4,500 RPM, requiring higher fueling at high engine speeds while requiring fuel trimming at low RPM to prevent rich fouling.
  • False Knock Detection: Aggressive cam lobes, mechanical lifters, and stiffer valve springs generate elevated acoustic valvetrain vibration, which factory knock sensors can misinterpret as spark knock.
  • Premature WOT Enrichment: Lower manifold vacuum can trick the ECU into triggering part-throttle and WOT fuel enrichments at gentle cruising speeds.

Tuner's Field Checklist: Camshaft Tuning

  1. Raise warm target idle speed (IDLRPM) by +50 to +150 RPM (approx 850–920 RPM) to stabilize vacuum and oil pressure.
  2. Add 2–4 degrees of spark advance in the idle region of HOTMAP to improve combustion stability with diluted overlap mixtures.
  3. Raise RPMMAP by +200 to +300 RPM to prevent lower idle vacuum from triggering premature part-throttle enrichment.
  4. Monitor wideband AFR above 4,800 RPM and add 5% to 10% fueling in PEFTBL to feed increased high-RPM volumetric efficiency.

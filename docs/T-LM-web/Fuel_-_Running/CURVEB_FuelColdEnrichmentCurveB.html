1. Overview & Purpose

CURVEB_FuelColdEnrichmentCurveB is the long-term / steady-state warm-up enrichment curve applied as the engine warms from cold start up to normal operating temperature.

Chrysler divides cold engine running enrichment into three distinct curves: - Curve A (CURVEA): Short-term post-start enrichment that decays rapidly within 30–60 seconds after startup. - Curve B (CURVEB): Long-term coolant-temperature enrichment that tracks the actual physical warm-up of the engine block and coolant from ice cold up to ~140°F+. - Curve C (CURVEC): Intermediate transition enrichment curve.

CURVEB ensures that as the engine is driven during the first 5 to 10 minutes of warm-up, the air/fuel mixture remains sufficiently rich to compensate for incomplete fuel vaporization and cold intake port wall condensation.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

CURVEB is evaluated during warmed-up and cold running conditions: * Lookup Input: Current CoolantTemp. * Combination: Summed with the decayed outputs of Curve A and Curve C, scaled by barometric pressure (BAROCP), and applied as a master multiplier to base speed-density fuel.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Increasing Table Values (Richer Warm-Up):
  • Injects additional fuel during the extended warm-up period.
  • Real-world feel: Eliminates cold bucking, stumbling, or surging during light acceleration in the first few minutes of driving after a cold start.
  • Decreasing Table Values (Leaner Warm-Up):
  • Leans out fuel delivery during warm-up.
  • Real-world feel: Cleans up rich, sooty warm-up mixtures, saves fuel, and improves emissions, but if leaned too far, causes cold throttle hesitation.
  • Warm Operating Boundary:
  • Notice that by 0x9B (~120–140°F), CURVEB drops to zero (0x00), transitioning the engine to pure warmed-up speed-density fueling.

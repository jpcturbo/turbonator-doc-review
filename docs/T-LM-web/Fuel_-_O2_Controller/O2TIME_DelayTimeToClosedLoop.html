1. Overview & Purpose

O2TIME_DelayTimeToClosedLoop establishes the minimum warmup hold time that the ECU must wait after the engine has reached operating temperature (CLTEMP) before attempting to transition from Open-Loop into Closed-Loop fuel feedback control.

A cold narrow-band zirconia O2 sensor cannot generate an accurate or reliable voltage signal until its ceramic element reaches internal operating temperatures of at least 600Â°F (315Â°C). Entering closed-loop operation prematurely causes violent fueling oscillations, surging, or stalling because the ECU misinterprets the high-impedance resting voltage of an unheated sensor as an extreme rich or lean condition. O2TIME guarantees that the sensor heater and hot exhaust gases have had adequate time to bring the sensor up to full operational temperature.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

The closed-loop delay timer (Timer_CLTIME_ClosedLoopTimer) begins incrementing only when all of the following entry conditions are met: * Engine Running: The engine must be running above crank/stall speed. * Warmup Temperature Reached: Coolant temperature must equal or exceed CLTEMP_TempToStartCLTIMETimer (typically 70Â°F to 120Â°F). * Load / MAP Limits: Manifold pressure must remain below MAPDEC_NoCellUpdateAndO2CNTRHeldBelowThisMap. * Off-Idle / Timer Checks: Open-loop fraction timer must remain below LMTOLF2.

Once the timer accumulates ticks equal to O2TIME, the ECU executes CheckIfO2SensorDataIsValid. If the sensor voltage has broken out of the deadband (outside O2TLML to O2TLMH), bit 4 of BitFlags_81 is asserted and closed loop feedback activates.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Factory Calibration Value: 0x15 (21 decimal × 2.8 s ≈ 59 seconds).
  • Increasing O2TIME:
  • Effect: Lengthens the time the engine stays in open-loop warm-up mode after cold startup.
  • When to Use: Unheated 1-wire O2 sensors installed in long-tube headers or large-diameter aftermarket downpipes located further downstream from the exhaust valves. The extra distance requires longer time for exhaust heat to soak the sensor.
  • Decreasing O2TIME:
  • Effect: Causes closed-loop mode to engage sooner after startup.
  • When to Use: Modern heated 3-wire or 4-wire O2 sensors with dedicated, fast-acting ceramic heaters that achieve operating temperature in under 15â€“20 seconds.
  • Caution: Setting O2TIME too low with an unheated or sluggish sensor will induce lean stumbling and hesitation during drive-away.

5. Calibration Notes & System Dependencies

  • Hardware Independence: Regardless of whether a 1-wire, 3-wire, or 4-wire O2 sensor is fitted to the car, the 6803 ECU hardware has no electrical sense of heater circuit current. It relies purely on the software timer O2TIME combined with the dynamic voltage threshold checks in O2TLMH and O2TLML.

1. Overview & Purpose

O2HSWP_HighLimitForO2RampSwitching is the upper voltage switching threshold used by the closed-loop air/fuel ratio controller to detect when the exhaust mixture has transitioned to the RICH side of stoichiometric (14.7:1 AFR for gasoline).

A narrow-band zirconia oxygen sensor outputs a non-linear S-curve voltage: typically 0.1V when lean and 0.9V when rich, with an extremely steep cliff around 0.45V to 0.50V. When the sensor voltage climbs equal to or above O2HSWP, the ECU confirms that excess fuel is present in the exhaust gas and immediately initiates fuel enleanment ramps to drive the mixture back toward stoichiometry.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

O2HSWP is active whenever the fuel feedback loop is closed: * Closed-Loop Active: Bit 4 of BitFlags_81 is set (warm engine, delay expired, sensor valid). * Part-Throttle / Idle Cruise: Engine is operating within closed-loop MAP limits (not at WOT or under high-boost power enrichment). * Sensor Voltage Evaluation: Evaluated on every O2 sampling loop. If O2SensorVolts >= O2HSWP, branch to O2Signal_AboveToggle.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Pairing with O2LSWP: O2HSWP (0x19 ≈ 0.488V) works as a tight hysteresis pair with O2LSWP (0x18 ≈ 0.469V). This tight 19mV window ensures rapid, responsive switching around the narrow-band midpoint.
  • Raising O2HSWP (e.g., to 0x22 / 0.66V):
  • The sensor must reach a higher voltage before the ECU acknowledges a rich condition.
  • Real-World Effect: Shifts the average closed-loop stoichiometric target slightly richer (e.g., 14.3:1 â€“ 14.5:1 AFR), which can reduce lean misfire or improve tip-in smoothness on aggressive camshafts.
  • Lowering O2HSWP:
  • Narrowing the gap between high and low switch points makes the feedback loop twitchy, increasing susceptibility to electrical noise in the sensor wiring.

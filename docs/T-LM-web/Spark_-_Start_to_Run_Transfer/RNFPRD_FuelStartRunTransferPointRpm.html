1. Overview & Purpose

RNFPRD_FuelStartRunTransferPointRpm is the fuel injection start-to-run transfer RPM threshold.

During cranking, injectors deliver rich, fixed-duration priming pulses designed to wet the intake ports and cylinders so the engine can catch fire. Once the engine catches and spins up past RNFPRD (approximately 705 RPM), the ECU cuts off cranking fuel and switches to normal running fuel calculations (speed-density speed/MAP lookup). Grouped under Spark_-_Start_to_Run_Transfer\ in the template along with the spark transfer constants, RNFPRD synchronizes the fueling transition with the ignition transition.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

RNFPRD is evaluated in the fuel calculation loop when determining whether to deliver cranking pulse widths or speed-density running pulse widths: * If TimerOverflowsBetweenDistPulses < RNFPRD (engine speed > ~705 RPM): The ECU branches to execute running fuel logic.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Increasing the Value (Lower RPM Threshold):
  • Effect: Cuts off cranking enrichment at a lower RPM (e.g., 550 RPM).
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Prevents engine flooding on warm restarts or when running oversized fuel injectors. However, setting it too low on freezing cold starts can cut cranking enrichment before the engine is warm enough to run on speed-density fuel, causing it to die immediately after catching.
  • Decreasing the Value (Higher RPM Threshold):
  • Effect: Extends cranking enrichment to higher engine speeds (e.g., 850 RPM).
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Helps cold starts catch solidly by delivering rich fuel until the engine flares fully, but risks loading up spark plugs with excess raw fuel on warm restarts.

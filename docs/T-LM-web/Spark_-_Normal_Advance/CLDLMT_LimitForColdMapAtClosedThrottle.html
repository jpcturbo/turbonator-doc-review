1. Overview & Purpose

CLDLMT_LimitForColdMapAtClosedThrottle is a safety clamp constant that limits the maximum advance that the cold MAP table (CLDMAP) can produce while the engine is at closed throttle (idling or trailing throttle on overrun).

During cold warm-up, manifold vacuum can be erratic due to cold friction, high idle AIS air bypass, and hunting engine speeds. If the cold MAP table produces high advance numbers under deep manifold vacuum, cold closed-throttle timing could flare unpredictably, causing unstable cold idle flare, surging, or erratic deceleration. CLDLMT enforces a maximum ceiling on the cold MAP timing contribution when the throttle plates are shut.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

CLDLMT is enforced under the following conditions: 1. Engine is Cold: Coolant temperature is below TMPSWP. 2. Throttle is Closed (Idle / Overrun): Bit 7 of BitFlags_81 is 0 (closed-throttle state). 3. CLDMAP Output Exceeds CLDLMT: The advance value returned by CLDMAP is greater than CLDLMT.

If the throttle is opened off-idle (Bit 7 = 1), the clamp is bypassed and CLDMAP is used without limitation.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Increasing the Value (Higher Clamp):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Allows more cold MAP advance during closed-throttle idle and decel.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Can raise cold idle RPM and stabilize an engine with an aggressive camshaft that struggles to maintain cold idle without extra timing. However, setting it too high can cause high cold idle hang or surging as the engine warms up.
  • Decreasing the Value (Lower Clamp):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Restricts the cold MAP advance to a lower number at closed throttle.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Prevents cold idle speed runaway and smooths deceleration overrun, but if set too low, cold idle may become sluggish, unstable, or prone to stalling when dropping back to idle.

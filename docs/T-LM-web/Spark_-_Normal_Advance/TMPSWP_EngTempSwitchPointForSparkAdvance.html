1. Overview & Purpose

TMPSWP_EngTempSwitchPointForSparkAdvance is the primary coolant temperature threshold that divides the ECU's ignition timing logic into two distinct modes: Cold Engine Operation and Warm (Normal) Engine Operation.

Cold engines have lower combustion chamber temperatures, slower fuel vaporization, and slower flame propagation speeds, requiring a dedicated ignition advance strategy (CLDMAP) to maintain stability and warm up the engine quickly. Once the coolant reaches normal operating temperature, the ECU switches to its primary warm timing tables (HOTMAP and WOTMAP). TMPSWP defines the exact temperature where this transition occurs.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

TMPSWP is evaluated on every execution pass of the main spark advance routine (CalculateSparkAdvance_MM):

  • Evaluated Input: CoolantTemp (analog-to-digital reading of the Coolant Temperature Sensor, stored in RAM).
  • Cold Path (CoolantTemp < TMPSWP): The ECU branches to the cold MAP advance table (CLDMAP) and applies cold closed-throttle clamping (CLDLMT).
  • Warm Path (CoolantTemp >= TMPSWP): The ECU branches to warm spark advance logic, selecting between part-throttle (HOTMAP) and full-throttle (WOTMAP).

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Increasing the Value (Higher Temp Threshold):
  • What it does: Forces the engine to stay in cold-engine ignition mode longer into its warmup cycle.
  • Effect on Total Timing: Extends the use of CLDMAP. Total timing will follow cold advance tables until the engine reaches a higher coolant temperature.

  • Real-World Driving Feel: Useful if an engine exhibits cold-stumble or hesitation during extended warm-ups, but setting it too high causes sluggish throttle response and poor fuel economy once the vehicle is being driven.

  • Decreasing the Value (Lower Temp Threshold):

  • What it does: Causes the ECU to switch into warm-engine spark logic earlier during warm-up.
  • Effect on Total Timing: Engages HOTMAP and WOTMAP sooner after startup.

  • Real-World Driving Feel: Can improve throttle response earlier during warm-up, but if set too low (e.g., below the thermostat opening temperature), a cold engine may encounter poor drivability or detonation under load due to insufficient warmup timing.

  • Typical Tuning Practice:

  • In stock calibrations, TMPSWP is set to 0xA6 (~166°F / 74°C), matching the factory 180°F thermostat warm-up curve. If running a cooler thermostat (such as a 160°F unit), tuners occasionally lower this constant slightly so the ECU consistently enters warm-engine spark mode.

5. Calibration Notes & System Dependencies

  • Coupled Fuel Switch: Note that the fuel system also has its own temperature thresholds for closed-loop O2 operation and enrichment. Adjusting TMPSWP alters ignition timing transitions independently of fuel transitions.
  • Hysteresis: There is no hysteresis band on TMPSWP itself; it is a single-byte unsigned comparison. Once coolant crosses this threshold, the warm spark path is taken immediately.

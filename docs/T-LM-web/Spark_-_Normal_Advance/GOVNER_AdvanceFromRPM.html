1. Overview & Purpose

GOVNER_AdvanceFromRPM is the primary engine speed advance curve (analogous to the mechanical / centrifugal advance weights in a traditional distributor).

In internal combustion engines, the time available for the air/fuel charge to burn decreases proportionally as engine RPM increases. To ensure that cylinder pressure peaks at the optimal mechanical angle (~14–17° after top dead center), the spark plug must fire earlier in the compression stroke at higher engine speeds. GOVNER forms the core foundation of total ignition timing across the entire operating range, to which MAP load advance (HOTMAP or WOTMAP) is added.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

GOVNER is active during all warmed-up running conditions (both part throttle and full throttle): * Execution Point: Line 6656 of CalculateSparkAdvance_MM. * Combination: The advance looked up from GOVNER is directly added to the load-based advance candidate in Temp0:

Intermediate Advance = GOVNER(RPM) + Temp0(MAP)

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

Because GOVNER is an additive baseline across all load zones, changes to GOVNER shift ignition timing across both part throttle and wide-open throttle:

  • Increasing Table Values (Adding RPM Advance):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Advances timing across the specified RPM range for all throttle positions and boost levels.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Sharpens throttle response, improves high-RPM power, and increases overall torque.
  • Cautions: Because it affects both WOT and cruising, advancing GOVNER without compensating in WOTMAP can push WOT boost timing into detonation.
  • Decreasing Table Values (Pulling RPM Advance):
  • Effect on Total Timing: Retards baseline timing across the board.
  • Real-World Driving Feel: Causes softer engine response and higher exhaust gas temperatures (EGTs).
  • Idle Stability:
  • At idle speeds (800–1,200 RPM), GOVNER sets the idle spark timing. A steeper timing slope around the idle target helps naturally stabilize idle RPM (engine dips -> timing advances -> torque increases to recover RPM).

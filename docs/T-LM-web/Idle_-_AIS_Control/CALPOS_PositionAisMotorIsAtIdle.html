1. Overview & Purpose

CALPOS (PositionAisMotorIsAtIdle) represents the electrical baseline step count of the AIS stepper motor during steady-state, fully warmed-up curb idle.

In the Chrysler idle control architecture, closed-loop idle trims and deceleration dashpot adders are computed as delta offsets relative to a baseline reference. CALPOS provides this nominal base (calibrated at 26 steps / 0x1A). It is added to desired position calculations and is also utilized in CalculateAisPosition_MM to enforce minimum limits and initialize the idle motor tracking register.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Evaluated in CalculateTargetIdleSpeedAndAisPosition_MM at line 5277 and CalculateAisPosition_MM at line 3636.
  • Added to cumulative step demand when closed-loop idle control is stable (adda CALPOS_PositionAisMotorIsAtIdle).
  • Checked in CalculateAisPosition_MM to clamp minimum position.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Base Throttle Minimum Airflow Setup: The factory service manual instructs setting the mechanical throttle plate minimum stop screw (with AIS motor disconnected or fully closed) so that warm idle runs ~700 RPM. When reconnecting the AIS, the ECU operates around CALPOS (~20 to 30 steps) to achieve 850 RPM target idle.
  • Aftermarket Throttle Bodies: If you install a larger throttle body or bore out the throttle body, the engine may idle at 1,100 RPM even with the AIS fully seated. After adjusting the mechanical throttle stop screw to bring baseline air down, verify that warm idle step counts in scan tools (or DRB) float close to CALPOS (20-30 steps).
  • Modifying CALPOS: Adjusting CALPOS shifts the entire baseline operating window of the idle stepper motor.

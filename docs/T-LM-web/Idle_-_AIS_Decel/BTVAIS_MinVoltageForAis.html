1. Overview & Purpose

BTVAIS (MinVoltageForAis) is a critical electrical protection threshold that guards the AIS stepper motor and ECU hardware against low system voltage.

The Chrysler AIS unit is a 4-wire bipolar stepper motor that draws substantial coil current through the ECU's internal quad-driver circuitry. If system voltage collapses (e.g. during heavy starter cranking, dead battery, or broken alternator belt), pulsing the stepper motor coils can draw enough current to drag the ECU's internal 5V regulated logic rail down, causing a microprocessor brownout reset. Furthermore, below 10.5V, the motor coils cannot generate sufficient magnetic torque to reliably overcome pintle spring tension, causing the rotor to skip steps and lose position synchronization. BTVAIS freezes the stepper motor in place whenever BatteryVolts < BTVAIS.

2. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

  • Evaluated inside subroutine CheckVoltageforAIS at line 5379.
  • Compares BatteryVolts against BTVAIS.
  • If BatteryVolts < BTVAIS (bcs LE730), the routine immediately exits, bypassing all stepper motor H-bridge coil driving logic.

3. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Stock Value: 0x71 (113 decimal = approximately 10.5 Volts).
  • Low-Voltage Tuning / Relocated Batteries: Do not lower BTVAIS below 10.0V. If an aftermarket trunk-mounted battery or undersized racing battery causes voltage sag during cranking, keeping BTVAIS at stock ensures the ECU does not lose stepper step count synchronization.

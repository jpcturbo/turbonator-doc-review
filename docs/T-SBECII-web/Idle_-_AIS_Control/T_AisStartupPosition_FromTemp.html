1. Overview & Purpose

AISTRT is a 2D calibration curve that establishes the open-loop baseline step position for the automatic idle speed stepper motor actuator during engine cranking and initial startup flare. Internal combustion engines exhibit significantly higher mechanical pumping friction and fuel condensation when cold, requiring substantial auxiliary bypass airflow to catch reliably and flare cleanly to starting idle speed. Before the engine starts and before closed-loop idle feedback can begin, the SBEC-II references AISTRT using engine coolant temperature to pre-position the stepper pintle. In factory calibrations, AISTRT commands approximately 134 open steps during extreme sub-zero cold starts, tapering down through 90 steps at freezing, 50 steps at mild temperatures, and settling at 30 steps when the engine is fully warmed up to 200 °F.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cold Cranking Stumble & Die-Out: * If the engine catches during cranking but stalls out immediately before entering run mode, increase AISTRT steps by 10 to 15 steps in the corresponding coolant temperature cells.
  • Taming Excessive Start Flare: * If cold starts flare aggressively above 2000 RPM, reduce AISTRT step values to limit the initial air surge.
  • Large Throttle Bodies: * Aftermarket 52mm or 58mm throttle bodies flow significantly more air per step than factory units; reduce AISTRT table values by 15% to 25% to retain factory startup manners.

1. Overview & Purpose

EGR19 (ExhGasRecircConstLoLimit) establishes the lower throttle position threshold for the Phase 2 Exhaust Gas Recirculation diagnostic flow verification window (Fault Code 32). During Phase 2, the engine controller commands the EGR valve closed and observes whether closed-loop oxygen sensor feedback shifts lean in response. For this shift to be detected reliably, the engine must produce enough exhaust gas volume and intake airflow for EGR shutoff to create a measurable change. At line 5495, relative throttle opening is checked against EGR19. The factory setting of 8 counts corresponds to approximately 0.16 volts (roughly 3 percent throttle opening), slightly higher than the Phase 1 entry gate, ensuring that the engine remains under positive load throughout the verification phase.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Raising EGR19: Enforces higher minimum throttle opening during Phase 2 flow verification. Enhances diagnostic signal-to-noise ratio on high-overlap camshaft setups where exhaust backpressure at light throttle is weak.
  • Lowering EGR19: Permits Phase 2 testing at lighter pedal angles. Avoid lowering below 5 counts to prevent coast-down deceleration from disrupting the oxygen sensor comparison.
  • Phase 1 Coordination: Factory setting (8 counts) is deliberately calibrated slightly higher than Phase 1 (EGR14, 5 counts) to ensure robust engine load during the active shutoff test.
  • Stock Setting: 8 counts (approx 0.16 volts / ~3 percent throttle) is optimal for production engines.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during warm, part-throttle cruising, modulating the exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) solenoid duty cycle to control combustion temperatures.

1. Overview & Purpose

DLYRPM defines the engine speed threshold that switches the stationary closed-throttle dashpot delay between the standard low-RPM timer DLYTIM2 and the extended high-RPM timer DLYTM3. When the accelerator pedal is released at a standstill, the severity of the deceleration depends heavily on engine speed. If the engine was merely idling or gently revved below DLYRPM, a brief dashpot delay (DLYTIM2) is sufficient to catch the engine smoothly. However, if the engine was revved to high RPM above DLYRPM, the extended dashpot delay (DLYTM3) is required to ensure safe recovery. In factory calibrations, DLYRPM is set to 94 counts, corresponding to an engine speed threshold of approximately 1504 RPM (nominally 1500 RPM).

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Split Point Optimization: * Raise DLYRPM to 1800 to 2000 RPM to keep light throttle blips quick and responsive on the shorter DLYTIM2 timer.
  • Camshaft Stall Buffer: * If an aggressive camshaft tends to stall when revved past 1400 RPM, keep DLYRPM at or below 1500 RPM so extended dashpot protection engages early.
  • Scaling Mechanics: * 1 count = 16 RPM on the 8-bit scale (94 counts = 1504 RPM).

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect when the throttle snaps shut at low vehicle speed, switching between standard and extended idle dashpot hold timers based on engine RPM.

1. Overview & Purpose

MAPFLR is a 2D calibration curve that defines an engine-speed-dependent manifold absolute pressure floor added to the deceleration control calculation. As engine RPM rises, the cylinders pump a vastly greater volume of air per unit time, resulting in deeper intake manifold vacuum when the throttle plate closes. To provide appropriate dashpot airflow across the entire RPM operating range, the calculated decel control manifold pressure must scale upward with engine speed. MAPFLR is indexed by engine RPM and establishes the base RPM-dependent manifold pressure adder, which is then multiplied by coolant temperature factor MPTEMP and adjusted for barometric pressure by BARFLR. In factory calibrations, MAPFLR commands 0 psi offset at 1100 RPM, scaling up to approximately 2.3 psi absolute (4.7 inHg) at 1500 RPM and above.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • High-RPM Decel Stall Prevention: * Increase MAPFLR points above 1500 RPM if the engine stalls during high-speed clutch disengagements.
  • Crisper High-RPM Shifts: * Lower MAPFLR points if the engine hangs or revs linger during high-RPM gear shifts, restoring rapid engine decel between gears.
  • Interaction with MPTEMP: * MAPFLR output is scaled by MPTEMP, so coolant temperature directly modulates how much of this adder is applied.

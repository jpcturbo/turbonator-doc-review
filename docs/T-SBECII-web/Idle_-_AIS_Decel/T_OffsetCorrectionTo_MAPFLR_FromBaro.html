1. Overview & Purpose

BARFLR is a 2D calibration curve that applies an altitude-dependent barometric pressure correction to the deceleration control manifold pressure calculation. At higher altitudes, atmospheric pressure is substantially lower than at sea level. Because manifold absolute pressure is measured relative to absolute vacuum, closed-throttle overrun at high elevation produces much lower absolute MAP readings than at sea level. Without barometric compensation, the calculated decel control MAP would become misaligned with actual intake manifold pressure, either causing false dashpot opening steps or inadequate deceleration cushioning. BARFLR provides a barometric trim adder to keep the decel control MAP threshold properly calibrated across varying altitudes. In factory calibrations, BARFLR is configured as a single baseline point that maintains zero offset under standard atmospheric pressure.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • High-Altitude Decel Hang: * If decel idle floats or hangs only at high elevations, trim BARFLR downward to lower the control MAP threshold in thin air.
  • Sea-Level Baseline: * Retain factory setting (0 offset) for normal sea-level and moderate elevation environments.
  • Companion Verification: * Works in parallel with BARAIS, which adjusts idle stepper motor opening steps for barometric pressure.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

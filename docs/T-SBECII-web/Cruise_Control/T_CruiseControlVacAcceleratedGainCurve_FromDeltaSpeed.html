1. Overview & Purpose

VACACC establishes the high-authority accelerated vacuum servo gain curve deployed when large speed deficits occur or when the driver engages the resume function. Under standard steady cruising, the controller utilizes moderate proportional gain to avoid abrupt throttle movements. However, when resuming a saved highway cruising speed from a significantly lower speed, or when an aggressive hill causes vehicle speed to drop substantially below the target, the controller sets an accelerated cruise flag and switches to VACACC. This curve commands substantially wider vacuum solenoid duty cycles to rapidly pull open the throttle plate and return the vehicle briskly to its target setpoint.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Resume Responsiveness: * Increasing VACACC delivers faster acceleration when the driver taps the resume switch from a lower road speed. * Useful for heavier vehicles or taller overdrive gearing where factory resume feels lethargic.
  • Overshoot Suppression: * If the vehicle surges past the target setpoint by several miles per hour upon resuming, reduce the upper points of VACACC. * Gated by ACLRST: once road speed is within 4.5 mph of setpoint, the controller drops out of accelerated cruise and returns to VACREG.
  • Aftermarket Throttle Bodies: * Larger 52mm or 58mm throttle bodies flow significantly more air at moderate openings; soften VACACC to maintain OEM-like composure.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect while cruise control is engaged with a large speed deficit or during Resume, applying accelerated vacuum servo pulses to pull vehicle speed back up rapidly.

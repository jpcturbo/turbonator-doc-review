1. Overview & Purpose

HOTMAP defines the primary warm part-throttle manifold pressure spark advance curve used when the engine is fully warmed up and operating at partial throttle. In Chrysler speed-density engine management, ignition timing is decomposed into an engine speed component and a manifold load component. Under light-load cruising with deep manifold vacuum, the cylinder air-fuel charge is lean and low in density, burning substantially slower than a dense wide-open throttle mixture. To achieve optimal combustion efficiency and fuel economy, HOTMAP commands high advance (up to 15 degrees BTDC) under deep vacuum, progressively reducing timing toward atmospheric pressure and transitioning into retard (down to -8 degrees BTDC at 14.7 psi boost) as manifold pressure builds.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cruising Efficiency & Throttle Snap: * Increasing timing in HOTMAP throughout the light-load vacuum range improves fuel mileage and sharpens engine response during light pedal tip-in.
  • Boost Transition Caution: * Avoid excessive advance in the 0 to 5 psi boost region; part-throttle boost knock often occurs here when climbing grades in higher gears.
  • WOTMAP Floor Relationship: * The firmware clamps part-throttle timing so that it cannot drop below the full-throttle curve (WOTMAP), ensuring a smooth and predictable timing transition as the driver floors the accelerator.
  • 3D Surface Synthesis (PTTIME): * In MPTune, HOTMAP is visually synthesized with GOVNER to produce the full 3D part-throttle spark table (PTTIME).

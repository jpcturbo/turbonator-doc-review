1. Overview & Purpose

RPMLMT defines an RPM-indexed upper limit ceiling on warm part-throttle manifold pressure spark advance. Under light throttle and deep manifold vacuum at low engine speeds, the theoretical mathematical sum of mechanical advance and part-throttle vacuum advance could command excessive total timing that causes low-RPM bucking, surging, or misfires. RPMLMT caps the maximum positive advance that HOTMAP can produce as a function of engine speed, ensuring that part-throttle timing ramps up in a controlled, smooth progression as engine speed rises.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Low-RPM Drivability Smoothing: * Lowering RPMLMT below 2500 RPM prevents excessive vacuum advance, eliminating light-throttle bucking or jerking.
  • Throttle Response Improvement: * Raising RPMLMT allows the engine to pull more vacuum advance during off-idle transitions, sharpening low-end engine response.
  • Capping Part-Throttle Advance: * Functions specifically as an upper boundary on HOTMAP, ensuring advance increases smoothly with RPM.

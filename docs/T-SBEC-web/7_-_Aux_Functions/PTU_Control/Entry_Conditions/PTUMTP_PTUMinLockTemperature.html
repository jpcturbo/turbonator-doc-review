1. Overview & Purpose

PTUMTP defines the minimum engine coolant temperature threshold required to permit Part Throttle Unlock (PTU) torque converter clutch modulation. The Part Throttle Unlock system was adapted from the throttle body injection and V6 engine controllers to control the automatic transmission torque converter lockup clutch during part-throttle cruising. When automatic transmission fluid and engine coolant are cold, fluid viscosity is thick and hydraulic line pressures are high, making converter clutch apply and release harsh. In factory calibrations, PTUMTP is set to 193 counts, corresponding to approximately 147 °F. Until the engine warms above this temperature, the controller holds the torque converter locked and inhibits part-throttle unlock cycling.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cold Driveline Protection: * Raise PTUMTP to 160 °F to 170 °F to prevent harsh lockup cycling on thick transmission fluid.
  • Aftermarket Torque Converters: * High-stall lockup converters with aggressive friction linings benefit from higher temperature thresholds to avoid shudder.
  • Warmup Behavior: * Below PTUMTP, the transmission remains locked in gear once engaged, bypassing throttle-induced unlock.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect when automatic transmission fluid and engine coolant are cold, fluid viscosity is thick and hydraulic line pressures are high, making converter clutch apply and release harsh.

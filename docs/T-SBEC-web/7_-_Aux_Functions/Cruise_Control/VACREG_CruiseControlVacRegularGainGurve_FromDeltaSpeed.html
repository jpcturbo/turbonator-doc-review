1. Overview & Purpose

VACREG defines the primary proportional vacuum servo gain curve utilized during steady-state cruising when road speed falls below the target setting. Under cruising conditions, aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, and highway grades create fluctuating loads. When vehicle speed drops below the set target, the engine controller calculates the positive speed deficit in miles per hour and queries this curve to determine the base vacuum solenoid pulse width. Applying engine manifold vacuum to the cruise servo diaphragm pulls the throttle cable open proportionally, overcoming hill climbs and headwind gusts to bring the vehicle smoothly back to the desired cruising speed without abrupt surging or hunting.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Countering Uphill Speed Sag: * Increasing the upper points of VACREG delivers stronger vacuum servo pull when vehicle speed drops on highway grades. * Recommended if the car consistently drops 2 to 4 mph below the target setpoint on moderate inclines.
  • Preventing Throttle Hunting: * Excessive gain causes rapid oscillations where the vehicle surges and backs off rhythmically on flat asphalt. * If throttle pedal pulsing occurs during steady cruise, soften the lower slope between 0 and 4 mph delta speed.
  • Turbocharger Vacuum Reservoir Considerations: * Under extended hill climbs, manifold vacuum drops toward zero as the turbocharger spools; verify the cruise vacuum reservoir and one-way check valve are airtight.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during steady-state cruising, pulsing the vacuum servo to maintain vehicle road speed on minor highway grades.

1. Overview & Purpose

BROPTM defines the pneumatic stabilization delay after the barometric read solenoid is energized before the engine controller reads the manifold absolute pressure sensor voltage. When the baro solenoid is energized, it closes the port connecting the sensor to intake manifold vacuum and opens a vent to underhood atmospheric air. Because air must physically flow through the vacuum harness to fill the vacuum line and sensor cavity up to atmospheric pressure, an instantaneous reading would measure residual vacuum, falsely recording lower barometric pressure. In factory calibrations, BROPTM is set to 9 counts, providing approximately 0.10 seconds (100 milliseconds) of pressure equalization time before sampling.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Long Vacuum Hose Routing: * Raise BROPTM to 14 to 18 counts (150 to 200 ms) if relocated pressure sensors use extended vacuum lines.
  • Pneumatic Dampener Installations: * Inline orifice restrictors or filters slow venting; increase BROPTM to allow complete atmospheric equalization.
  • Under-Reporting Prevention: * Never set BROPTM below 6 counts (~60 ms) to avoid sampling lingering manifold vacuum.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect during in-flight baro reading cycles, setting the pneumatic settling delay after switching the baro solenoid open.

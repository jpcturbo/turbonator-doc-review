1. Overview & Purpose

AIRTMP defines the intake air charge temperature fuel compensation curve. Ambient and compressed air density varies inversely with temperature: cold air is dense and contains more oxygen molecules per volume, while hot compressed charge air is less dense. AIRTMP reads the raw intake charge temperature sensor voltage (where higher voltage represents colder air and lower voltage represents hot compressed air) and scales fuel pulsewidth accordingly. This table provides weather compensation across freezing winter and blistering summer conditions, as well as compensating for turbocharger intercooler heat soak.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Cold Weather Density Compensation: * Increases fueling in freezing ambient conditions to prevent lean combustion from dense winter air.
  • Intercooler Heat Soak Trimming: * Reduces pulsewidth under hot charge air conditions (low sensor voltage), preventing rich bogging on consecutive boost pulls.
  • Inverted Voltage Axis: * Sensor voltage is inversely proportional to temperature: high voltage (4.0 to 5.0 Volts) = cold air; low voltage (0.0 to 1.5 Volts) = hot air.
  • Lineage from T2 Calibration: * Copied from the factory 1989 T2 calibration into T-SBEC to support charge temperature sensor equipped engines.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect continuously across all running conditions, adjusting fuel delivery based on intake air charge temperature to compensate for air density changes.

1. Overview & Purpose

PTENR2 sets the distributor reference pulse countdown interval governing the decay rate of part-throttle enrichment when manifold pressure falls below the RPMMAP boundary curve. When the driver modulates or lifts off the throttle after accelerating in boost, the engine controller must taper off transient enrichment smoothly to prevent sudden lean surges or unnecessary fuel waste. PTENR2 defines how many engine position pulses must occur before the controller decrements the open-loop fraction timer by the negative step value in PTENRD. The factory setting of 61 pulses ensures that transient fuel tapers down gradually over approximately 30 engine revolutions on a four-cylinder engine.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Faster Fuel Decay: Lowering the pulse countdown value causes part-throttle enrichment to decay away more rapidly, returning the engine to standard closed-loop or baseline fueling faster to improve fuel economy.
  • Sustained Post-Boost Fueling: Raising the pulse countdown value sustains enrichment longer after lifting off the throttle, which helps prevent lean popping in the exhaust during rapid gear shifts or when feathering the throttle in road-course driving.
  • Coordination with PTENRD: Coordinate changes to PTENR2 with the step decrement value in PTENRD to control total decay curve slope.
  • Drivability Tuning: Set high enough to prevent lean bucking when rolling off the throttle at highway speeds.

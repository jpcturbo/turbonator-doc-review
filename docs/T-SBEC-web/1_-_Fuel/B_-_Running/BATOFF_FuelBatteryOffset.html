1. Overview & Purpose

BATOFF defines the fuel injector dead time latency offset curve indexed against electrical system battery voltage. Electromagnetic fuel injectors require a measurable time interval (dead time, typically 0.6 to 1.5 milliseconds) for the solenoid coil to build magnetic flux and pull the pintle off its seat before fuel begins spraying. As battery voltage decreases, solenoid opening velocity slows down significantly. BATOFF adds an offset pulsewidth that grows larger as system voltage sags, ensuring that effective fuel delivery remains constant regardless of alternator output or electrical accessories cycling on.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Aftermarket Injector Characterization: * Populate BATOFF with manufacturer-supplied voltage offset data when upgrading injectors to ensure stable air-fuel ratios.
  • Electrical Load Cycling Diagnosis: * If idle air-fuel ratio dips rich or spikes lean when cooling fans cycle on, adjust BATOFF at the 12.0 to 13.5 Volt region.
  • Low-Pulsewidth Linearity: * At idle and low load, dead time represents a large percentage of total pulsewidth; precise BATOFF calibration is vital for idle stability.
  • Failsafe Voltage Backup: * If battery voltage reading is flagged faulty, the ECU automatically falls back to a 14.1 Volt default calibration index.

1. Overview & Purpose

AISLML defines the lower step limit offset subtracted from baseline idle position to clamp the minimum opening of the idle stepper motor during rolling idle mode. While the vehicle is rolling in neutral with closed throttle, the engine relies on auxiliary bypass airflow to avoid sudden stalling when the brakes are applied or when clutching back into gear. If the closed-loop controller observed a minor overspeed and drove the stepper motor completely shut, the engine would be left with zero bypass air, causing an immediate stall when vehicle speed halted. AISLML establishes a protective lower floor: the desired motor position during rolling idle is never permitted to drop below baseline steps minus AISLML. In factory calibrations, AISLML is set to 4 counts, ensuring the stepper motor cannot close more than 4 steps below the calibrated baseline during rolling idle.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Coasting Undershoot Protection: * Keep AISLML between 2 and 4 steps to guarantee that the idle stepper motor cannot choke off bypass air while rolling to a stop.
  • Aggressive Camshaft Floor: * Lower AISLML to 1 or 2 steps on high-overlap camshafts to ensure rolling idle never drops below the baseline airflow floor.
  • Excessive Travel Warning: * Avoid setting AISLML higher than 8 steps, as the controller can pull the motor shut on rolling overspeeds, inducing stalls.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

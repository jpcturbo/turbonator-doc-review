1. Overview & Purpose

FANBAT defines the underhood temperature threshold measured at the battery tray thermistor that triggers radiator cooling fan activation. In turbocharged Chrysler vehicles, high underhood radiant heat from the turbocharger turbine housing and exhaust manifold can accumulate in the engine compartment during low-speed driving or after hard boost runs. Because the battery and the engine controller power module share the engine compartment behind the left headlight, extreme heat degrades lead-acid battery life and reduces alternator voltage regulation efficiency. In factory calibrations, FANBAT is set to 27 counts, corresponding to approximately 120 °F. If battery temperature rises above this threshold, the controller energizes the cooling fan to evacuate hot air from the engine bay.

2. Tuner's Guide & Real-World Calibration

  • Tight Engine Bay Heat Management: * Lower FANBAT to 110 °F to 115 °F on cramped L-body and G-body turbo cars to purge radiant exhaust heat.
  • Battery Relocation Safeguard: * If the battery is relocated to the trunk and the thermistor is deleted, set FANBAT high to prevent false fan triggers.
  • Sensor Inversion Note: * The battery thermistor uses negative temperature coefficient logic; lower sensor voltage counts represent higher physical temperature.

3. Operating Conditions (When Does This Take Effect?)

Takes effect when underhood battery tray temperature rises above this threshold, turning the radiator cooling fan ON to flush stagnant hot air from the engine bay.
